1.4 Quarter-Defining Events

QuarterThe Story Behind the Numbers
2T25A clean operational quarter that doubled adjusted profit. Distribution carried the period with broad-based EBITDA growth across subsidiaries — Amapá (+92%), Rio Grande do Sul (+42%) and Piauí (+24%) all posted standout gains — while a one-off R$444 million favorable PPA adjustment tied to the resolution of long-running Equatorial Goiás legal contingencies inflated reported EBITDA to R$3.85bn. The Sabesp stake contributed R$312mn via equity income, already signalling the growing strategic weight of that holding.
3T25Operational momentum continued with the eighth consecutive quarter of energy losses below the regulatory ceiling, underscoring the maturity of the distribution turnaround playbook. However, the renewables segment began showing cracks: Echoenergia's wind complexes recorded a 5.2% YoY drop in wind speed, the first clear sign of the curtailment and resource-variability issue that would dominate the next two quarters. Sanitation revenue fell 37.9% YoY, though this reflects construction-revenue accounting rather than underlying concession deterioration.
4T25The most complex quarter of the cycle: adjusted figures beat expectations comfortably (EBITDA +10.5%, adjusted net income 12% above Safra's estimate), but the company simultaneously recognized R$3.547bn in impairments — R$3.239bn on Echoenergia and R$309mn on the Amapá sanitation concession (CSA) — alongside a R$2.2bn capital gain from the sale of transmission assets. The net result was a consolidated net loss of R$102mn against a R$1.503bn profit a year earlier, even as the underlying distribution business posted an 18% EBITDA increase. Management also proposed cutting the mandatory dividend payout to 1% of adjusted net income, a structural signal that capital will be redirected toward the investment cycle rather than distributions.
1T26A "steady distribution, struggling renewables" quarter that set the tone for 2026. Distribution again outperformed, but Echoenergia's EBITDA fell 34.1% YoY to R$88.1mn as curtailment persisted (albeit less severely than in prior quarters) and the energy-trading book recorded a negative mark-to-market. The 23.6% YoY decline in adjusted net income — despite the EBITDA beat — was driven by a 21% jump in depreciation and a 10% increase in net financial expense, both consequences of the heavy 2025 investment cycle and Brazil's elevated Selic rate. Leverage held at a controlled 2.7x net debt/EBITDA (3.1x excluding the transmission sale gain), and the Sabesp equity contribution rose to R$254mn, up R$40mn YoY.

1.5 Recurring Earnings Patterns

#Structural Observation
1Distribution is the consistent EBITDA engine: in all four quarters, the segment posted double-digit YoY EBITDA growth (+23% in 2T25, strong in 3T25, +18% in 4T25, robust again in 1T26), anchored by tariff resets, volume growth and continued reduction of energy losses below regulatory thresholds.
2Renewables (Echoenergia) is the perennial drag: wind-speed shortfalls and ANEEL-imposed curtailment reduced segment EBITDA in 3T25, 4T25 and 1T26, culminating in a R$3.239bn impairment in 4T25 and a further 34% EBITDA decline in 1T26.
3The Sabesp equity stake (15%) is becoming an increasingly material — and growing — contributor to net income, rising from R$312mn (2T25) to R$394mn (4T25) to R$254mn (1T26, +R$40mn YoY), reinforcing the strategic logic behind the investment.
4The gap between adjusted and GAAP consolidated net income widened sharply through the cycle, driven by escalating one-off items — a R$444mn favorable PPA adjustment (2T25), a R$2.2bn transmission-sale gain offset by R$3.547bn of impairments (4T25) — making the adjusted figure the more reliable proxy for recurring earnings power.
5Leverage improved structurally over the cycle (3.1x → 3.3x → 2.6x → 2.7x net debt/EBITDA), with the 4T25 transmission-asset sale providing a step-change deleveraging event even as the absolute capex run-rate stayed elevated at R$2.7–3.0bn per quarter.

2.1 Business Model & Revenue Architecture

Equatorial Energia is Brazil's third-largest power distribution group by customer count (≈14.6 million clients) and has evolved into a diversified "multi-utility" platform spanning power distribution, transmission, renewable generation, energy commercialization, sanitation and telecom services. The group's playbook — acquiring distressed regulated assets (frequently via privatization auctions) and executing aggressive operational turnarounds — remains the core engine of value creation. FY2025 consolidated net revenue (excluding construction revenue) reached R$41.367 billion (+17.4% YoY), with Societary EBITDA of R$11.276 billion (-1.0% YoY, reflecting renewables and sanitation drag against a strong distribution base).

2.3 Top 3 Risks

1. Renewables segment structural underperformance

HIGH · NEAR-TERM
Echoenergia's wind and solar assets have underperformed for at least three consecutive quarters, with cumulative curtailment-related effects of R$496 million accumulated since 2023 (split across grid-reliability constraints, energy-ratio effects and external unavailability). The 4T25 impairment of R$3.239 billion materially reduced the carrying value of these assets, and 1T26 EBITDA for the segment fell a further 34.1% YoY. Until wind resource and curtailment policy normalize, this segment will continue to act as a drag on consolidated EBITDA growth and could trigger further impairment reviews if conditions persist into 2H26.

2. Elevated leverage amid Brazil's high interest-rate environment

HIGH · ONGOING
Net debt stood at R$41.9-44.3 billion across the last two reported quarters, with leverage of 2.6x-3.1x net debt/EBITDA depending on whether the 4T25 transmission-sale gain is included. With Brazil's Selic rate at elevated levels through the period, net financial expense has been a recurring drag — growth of +10% to +13% YoY in financial expenses was cited as a primary driver of adjusted net income declines in both 4T25 and 1T26, even as EBITDA beat consensus in both quarters. A continuation of the heavy capex cycle (R$10.985bn invested in 2025, +23.5% YoY) without commensurate EBITDA growth from renewables and sanitation could pressure leverage ratios back toward the 3.3x peak seen in 3T25.

3. Regulatory and tariff risk across a multi-state distribution footprint

MEDIUM · MULTI-YEAR
Equatorial's distribution EBITDA is generated across seven separate regulatory jurisdictions (Maranhão, Pará, Piauí, Alagoas, Amapá, Rio Grande do Sul and Goiás), each subject to its own periodic tariff revision (RTP) and annual tariff adjustment (RTA) cycles, plus changes to ANEEL's nationwide Opex remuneration methodology (a change was approved in May 2025). While the company has historically navigated these cycles well — energy losses remain below regulatory ceilings and managed costs grew only modestly in 2025 — any unfavorable methodology change or adverse tariff outcome at one of the larger subsidiaries (e.g., Celpa/Pará or Cemar/Maranhão) would directly affect the segment that represents the large majority of group EBITDA.

Favorable

Price dislocation thesis. EQTL3 trades at R$38.77, roughly 17% below its 52-week high of R$46.32 and within a mid-range position despite four consecutive quarters of EBITDA beats versus consensus. The gap between the average sell-side price target (R$48.42) and the current price — approximately 25% — suggests the market is discounting the GAAP-level earnings volatility (the 4T25 net loss of R$102mn, driven by non-cash impairments) more heavily than the underlying adjusted earnings trend, which has consistently exceeded analyst EBITDA estimates.

Fundamental quality assessment. The core distribution franchise — representing the large majority of group EBITDA — remains in excellent operational health: energy losses have stayed below regulatory ceilings for eight consecutive quarters, default rates are improving, and tariff-driven EBITDA growth has been in the double digits in every one of the last four quarters. The Sabesp equity stake is emerging as a second, growing pillar of earnings quality. Leverage has improved meaningfully (3.3x to 2.6x-2.7x) following the transmission-asset divestment, providing balance-sheet flexibility that is relatively rare among Brazilian regulated utilities of this scale.

Primary risk acknowledgement. The renewables segment (Echoenergia) is the clearest near-term threat to the bull thesis: a R$3.239bn impairment in 4T25 was followed by a further 34.1% YoY EBITDA decline in 1T26, and cumulative curtailment-related effects since 2023 now total R$496mn. Combined with Brazil's high Selic rate inflating net financial expense — the primary culprit behind the 20.7% and 23.6% YoY declines in adjusted net income in 4T25 and 1T26 respectively, despite EBITDA beats in both periods — this creates a credible scenario in which "EBITDA beats, net income disappoints" persists through 2026, capping near-term multiple expansion even if the operational story remains intact.

Near-term catalysts. The 2T26 results (expected mid-August 2026) will be the key proximate catalyst, with particular attention to: (i) whether Echoenergia's EBITDA stabilizes or continues its double-digit decline; (ii) the trajectory of the Sabesp equity-income contribution; (iii) early evidence on how the revised dividend policy (1% of adjusted net income) is communicated and executed; and (iv) whether net debt/EBITDA continues trending toward the ~2.5x area or reverts higher as the elevated 2025 capex run-rate (R$10.985bn, +23.5% YoY) persists into 2026. A favorable combination of these factors — particularly any sign of renewables stabilization — would represent the most direct path toward closing the gap to the average analyst target.
