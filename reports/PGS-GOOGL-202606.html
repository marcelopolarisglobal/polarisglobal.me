1.4 Quarter-Defining Events — The Story Behind the Numbers

Q2 2025
The Capital Return Inflection

For years, the bear case on Alphabet's AI spending cycle was framed as a binary: invest or return cash, not both. Q2 2025 dismantled that framing in a single press release. Alongside a revenue and EPS beat ($96.43bn / $2.31, both ahead of Street), Alphabet declared its first-ever quarterly dividend of $0.21 per share and authorized a $70bn share buyback — while simultaneously raising its 2025 capex guidance by $10bn to approximately $85bn. The market's initial reaction was muted (shares moved roughly flat to slightly positive in after-hours trading), reflecting digestion of the higher spending figure, but the quarter set the template that would recur for the next year: beat-and-raise, with Cloud doing the heavy lifting. Cloud revenue of $13.62bn (+32% YoY) beat estimates by nearly $500mn, and the Cloud backlog climbed to $106bn, up 18% sequentially and 38% year-over-year, with management citing more than 85,000 businesses using Gemini.

Q3 2025
The $100 Billion Quarter, Clouded by Brussels

Q3 2025 delivered Alphabet's first-ever quarter above $100bn in revenue ($102.3bn, +16% YoY), with EPS of $2.87 up 35% year-over-year — yet the headline operating margin of 30.5% told only part of the story. A $3.5bn European Commission antitrust fine, recognized as a charge within Google Services operating income, mechanically depressed reported profitability; stripping it out, operating margin was a much healthier 33.9% and operating income grew 22% rather than the reported 9%. Investors largely looked through the charge: the stock jumped more than 6% in after-hours trading, part of a rally that had already pushed shares up over 30% in the preceding months. Cloud growth accelerated again to 34% YoY, and management raised full-year 2025 capex guidance to a $91–93bn range — the second consecutive quarter of an upward capex revision.

Q4 2025
A Record Year, and Capex Sticker Shock

Q4 2025 closed out a year in which Alphabet's annual revenue surpassed $400bn for the first time ($402.84bn, +15.1%), with the Gemini 3 launch cited by CEO Sundar Pichai as a "major milestone" and the Gemini app reaching 750 million monthly active users. Revenue of $113.83bn (+18% YoY) and EPS of $2.82 both beat consensus comfortably. But two threads complicated the picture: YouTube advertising revenue of $11.38bn missed the $11.84bn Street estimate — management attributed part of the softness to advertising headwinds — and operating income absorbed a $2.1bn employee compensation charge tied to Waymo. Most consequentially, Alphabet guided 2026 capital expenditures to $175–185bn, more than double the 2025 level, framed around AI compute demand for Google DeepMind and Cloud customers. Initial after-hours trading saw shares fall as much as 3% as investors digested the scale of the spending commitment, even against an otherwise record quarter.

Q1 2026
The AI Payoff Quarter — and a Higher Capex Bill to Match

Q1 2026 was the quarter the market concluded "the AI spending is paying off." Revenue accelerated again to $109.9bn (+22% YoY, the highest growth rate since 2022), Google Cloud revenue surged 63% to $20.0bn — its highest growth rate in the dataset — and Cloud operating margin expanded from 17.8% a year earlier to 32.9%, with Cloud operating income reaching $6.6bn. CEO Sundar Pichai told analysts that "enterprise AI solutions have become our primary growth driver for cloud for the first time," while also acknowledging the company remains "compute constrained in the near term" and that "cloud revenue would have been higher if we were able to meet the demand." Headline EPS of $5.11 (+82% YoY) was flattered by a $36.9bn gain on non-marketable equity securities — chiefly revaluations of Alphabet's stakes in Waymo and Anthropic — adding $2.35 to EPS; on a core basis, EPS of roughly $2.62 was approximately in line with consensus. The market reaction was unambiguous: shares rose 3.3% immediately and then surged 9.96% the following session to close at $384.80, even as management raised 2026 capex guidance again, to $180–190bn, and flagged that 2027 capex would "significantly increase" further.

#Observation
1Revenue growth has accelerated for four consecutive quarters — +14% (Q2 2025) → +16% (Q3 2025) → +18% (Q4 2025) → +22% (Q1 2026) — an unusual trajectory for a company of Alphabet's scale, driven primarily by Google Cloud's re-acceleration from +32% to +63% YoY over the same window.
2Google Cloud's operating margin has expanded dramatically and consistently, moving from roughly 17.8% in Q1 2025 toward 32.9% in Q1 2026 — converting what was historically a margin drag into a meaningful profit contributor and the single biggest swing factor in consolidated operating margin.
3Each of the last three quarters has carried a distinct one-time item that materially distorts headline operating income or EPS comparability: a legal settlement charge in Q2 2025, a $3.5bn EC antitrust fine in Q3 2025, a $2.1bn Waymo compensation charge in Q4 2025, and a $36.9bn non-marketable equity gain in Q1 2026. 4YouTube advertising has missed Street estimates in two of the last four quarters (Q4 2025 and Q1 2026), even as Search & other has consistently beaten or met expectations (+19% YoY in Q1 2026) — a bifurcation within Google Services worth monitoring as a leading indicator of advertiser budget allocation shifts.
5Management has raised full-year capex guidance at every single one of the last three earnings calls — to ~$85bn (Q2 2025), to $91–93bn (Q3 2025), to $175–185bn for 2026 (Q4 2025), and to $180–190bn for 2026 (Q1 2026) — a consistent pattern of upward revisions that has directly tracked Cloud demand but increasingly weighs on near-term free cash flow.

2.3 Top Risks

1. Capex Super-Cycle & Free Cash Flow Compression
High Severity

Alphabet has raised its capex guidance at every earnings call for the last four quarters, most recently to $180–190bn for 2026 (more than double the 2025 level), with management explicitly flagging that 2027 capex will "significantly increase" further. Consensus free cash flow estimates have responded accordingly: from an actual $73.3bn in 2025, FCF is projected to fall to approximately $20.5bn in 2026 (est., per TIKR's Actuals and Forward Estimates data) — a roughly 72% decline — before an estimated recovery toward $35.5bn in 2027 and $68.1bn in 2028 as depreciation cycles normalize. The company has already moved to fund part of this gap externally, completing an $84.75bn equity capital raise in early June 2026 (including a $10bn Berkshire Hathaway private placement) and pushing total debt above $100bn for the first time. The near-term risk is twofold: that capex continues to escalate beyond the current guidance without a commensurate near-term revenue contribution, and that further dilutive capital raises become necessary, both of which would weigh on per-share value even as the underlying business scales.

Severity: High  ·  Timeline: 2026–2028  ·  Status: Active, guidance raised most recently Apr 29, 2026
2. Regulatory, Antitrust & AI-Output Liability
Medium-High Severity

Alphabet's regulatory overhang has already produced cash consequences: a $3.5bn European Commission antitrust fine was charged against Q3 2025 operating income, and the company's annual report continues to disclose ongoing EU and US antitrust proceedings, including prior Shopping and Android decisions. More recently, a June 10, 2026 ruling found Google liable for false claims generated by its AI Overviews feature — an early instance of legal exposure tied directly to generative AI outputs, a category of risk that is likely to recur as AI Overviews and AI Mode scale across Search. Separately, the US DOJ search-remedies case remains a structural overhang: any requirement to alter default-search distribution agreements (the foundation of the Section 2.2 Search moat) would have first-order implications for Google Services economics. None of these items individually threatens the franchise, but their cumulative direction — more frequent, more AI-output-specific legal exposure layered on top of long-running antitrust matters — represents a structural cost and uncertainty overhang that is not fully resolved.

Severity: Medium-High  ·  Timeline: Ongoing, with the AI Overviews ruling dated Jun 10, 2026  ·  Status: Active across multiple jurisdictions
3. AI-Driven Disintermediation of Search & Advertising
Medium Severity

The bear case that generative AI assistants will divert query volume — and the advertising dollars that follow it — away from traditional Search remains unresolved, even though the data through Q1 2026 has been reassuring: Search & other revenue grew 19% YoY, and queries were described as being at an all-time high. However, two softer data points warrant attention. First, YouTube advertising has missed consensus in two of the last four quarters, which could reflect either advertiser-budget reallocation toward AI-native ad formats or simply normal estimate noise — the pattern is not yet conclusive but bears watching. Second, the competitive field has intensified materially: OpenAI, Anthropic, and others continue to scale consumer AI assistants that increasingly handle tasks (research, shopping comparisons, recommendations) that previously routed through a Google Search query. Alphabet's own Gemini ecosystem (750M MAU) is partly a defensive response to this dynamic. The risk is not imminent revenue impairment — current trends point the other way — but a multi-quarter erosion in query/ad intensity that would not be obvious until it shows up in Search & other growth deceleration.

Severity: Medium  ·  Timeline: 12–24 months  ·  Status: Monitoring; no confirmed deceleration in Search & other as of Q1 2026

Against a 63-analyst consensus price target of $432.83 — itself set largely before the equity raise's dilutive impact was fully digested — the current level implies approximately 21% upside to consensus, a gap that appears to reflect near-term digestion of dilution and capex-guidance shock rather than a reassessment of the underlying earnings trajectory, which has continued to accelerate through Q1 2026.

Fundamental quality assessment. The four-quarter sequence reviewed in Section 1 shows revenue growth accelerating in every quarter — from +14% to +22% YoY — driven by Google Cloud's transformation from a 32%-growth, margin-dilutive segment a year ago to a 63%-growth segment now posting a 32.9% operating margin. Search & other, the core of the franchise, has shown no visible deceleration (+19% YoY in Q1 2026) despite two years of AI-disruption commentary. The $460bn Cloud backlog provides multi-quarter revenue visibility that is unusually concrete for a company of this scale, and the FY2025 segment mix (Section 2.1) confirms that Cloud, while still only 14.6% of revenue, is now the primary marginal driver of both growth and margin expansion.

Primary risk acknowledgement. The dominant risk to this thesis is not competitive or regulatory in the near term — though both remain live, per Section 2.3 — but financial: the capex super-cycle to $180–190bn in 2026, with 2027 guided higher still, is projected to compress free cash flow from $73.3bn in 2025 to roughly $20.5bn in 2026 (est.), a decline the company has already begun funding through dilutive equity issuance and a sharply expanded debt load (now exceeding $100bn). Should the capex trajectory extend further without Cloud revenue realization keeping pace, or should the regulatory overhang (the DOJ search case, the June 2026 AI Overviews liability ruling, and ongoing EU antitrust matters) crystallize into structural remedies, the bear case in Section 2.5 becomes the operative scenario.

Near-term catalysts. The next reporting date of July 22, 2026 will be the first opportunity to observe (a) whether Cloud's 63% growth rate and $460bn backlog continue to build or begin normalizing, (b) the initial per-share impact of the June 2026 equity raise, (c) whether YouTube advertising returns to beating consensus after two consecutive misses, and (d) any further commentary on the TPU roadmap (Ironwood/Icefish) that underpins the long-term cost-structure argument for Cloud margins. Progress on Waymo's commercial rollout (the new Premier subscription tier and its 2026 World Cup advertising campaign) offers an additional, lower-weight catalyst on the Other Bets optionality embedded in the moat assessment.
