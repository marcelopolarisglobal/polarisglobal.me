1.4Quarter-defining events — the story behind the numbers

QuarterNarrative
2T25The cleanest beat of the window. Profit jumped 50% YoY, but the optical surge was partly a soft base: 2T24 had been scarred by the Rio Grande do Sul floods, so the YoY loss-ratio improvement (−2.1pp in Insurance) flattered the comparison. The genuine signal was the financial result more than doubling (+121%) as the treasury portfolio rolled into higher-yielding paper, and management raising full-year guidance — a rare mid-year upgrade that reset expectations higher for the back half.
3T25A "quality over slope" quarter. Headline growth decelerated to +12.6% and profit slipped −5.3% sequentially off the strong 2T25 base, which the market read as the first sign that the easy comparisons were over — hence the negative drift despite a modest consensus beat. Underneath, Health stole the show (revenue +27%, profit +65%) and the non-insurance verticals climbed to 44% of group profit, validating that the diversification engine was real rather than cyclical.
4T25The full-year capstone: FY2025 closed at R$ 41bn revenue (+12%) and R$ 3.4bn net income (+28%). The 4T25 print itself was the seasonally weakest of the year (ROAE 22.5%), and shares fell 2.9% as investors focused on competitive pressure in auto and questioned whether consortium revenue-recognition changes and credit-quality normalisation in the Bank could cap 2026 momentum. Porto Bank's 28.4% ROAE was the quiet standout.
1T26The diversification thesis fully landed — but with a tax asterisk. Recurring profit rose 15% to R$ 958m (fifth straight double-digit quarter), with Insurance profit up 49% and ROAE of 33.8% on a benign loss environment. The R$ 1.134bn accounting headline was lifted by an ISAR-incorporation tax credit, which management correctly stripped from recurring. The one blemish was the financial result, which fell 20% YoY as inflation-linked positions rolled at less favourable carry. Shares barely moved — the good news was already priced.

Section 2

Institutional Analysis

Porto is no longer a pure auto insurer; it is a financial ecosystem — insurance, health, a digital bank and services — distributed through Brazil's largest independent broker network. That broker channel is the moat the market keeps underwriting.

The architecture matters because it changes what kind of company PSSA3 is. For most of its history Porto was valued as a Brazilian auto insurer — a cyclical, weather- and pricing-exposed underwriter whose earnings rose and fell with loss ratios and reserve adequacy. The de-facto holding structure that has emerged over the last three years is different: a financial-services group where the lower-margin, slower-growing auto book funds customer acquisition and brand, while three faster, higher-incremental-ROE verticals (Health, Bank, Services) capture an expanding share of the same client's wallet. The strategic logic is a flywheel — the broker sells an auto policy, the relationship opens the door to a health plan, a credit card, a consortium quota and an assistance package, and each additional product both raises lifetime value and deepens the switching cost. The 1T26 disclosure that non-insurance verticals reached ~51% of group profit is the clearest evidence yet that the flywheel is turning under its own momentum rather than being pushed by a single strong quarter. The investment question is therefore less "where are auto loss ratios heading" and more "can Porto keep converting its distribution advantage into high-ROE non-insurance earnings faster than auto economics erode."

The single pillar that justifies the premium-to-peers narrative is distribution. Porto's independent broker network is the largest of its kind in Brazil, and it is the asset that direct-to-consumer and insurtech challengers have repeatedly failed to replicate at scale. Brokers are not merely a sales channel; they are a trust intermediary in a market where consumers are wary of fine print and claims disputes, and they are the mechanism through which Porto cross-sells across verticals without the customer-acquisition cost that a pure digital bank or health plan would bear. That advantage is real but not permanent — the secular risk is that pricing transparency and aggregator platforms gradually commoditise the auto purchase, weakening the broker's gatekeeping role in the highest-volume product. PGS rates the broader moat as wide-but-narrowing in auto and widening in the ecosystem: the more of a client's financial life Porto captures, the harder that client is to dislodge on price alone, which is precisely why management's cross-sell and high-ROE-mix strategy is also a defensive one.

2.3Top 3 risks

1 · Auto insurance commoditisation & price competition
HIGH
Auto is the legacy core and remains exposed to aggressive price competition and the slow structural decline of car ownership economics. January-2026 direct auto premiums fell 4.2% YoY with a 57.1% loss ratio. Fleet growth has slowed to ~3%. If competitors discount to win share, Porto faces a choice between defending margin (losing volume) or defending volume (compressing the combined ratio). Timeline: persistent, near-term sensitive.
2 · Bank credit-quality normalisation
MEDIUM
Porto Bank is the highest-ROAE growth engine but also the riskiest. Credit losses jumped 44% YoY in 1T26 as the loan book scaled into cards, financing and consortium. Full-year 2026 guidance caps credit losses at ~R$3.1bn. In a higher-for-longer Selic environment with stretched Brazilian household debt, a faster-than-guided deterioration would directly dent the diversification narrative. Timeline: 2026–2027.
3 · Financial-result & macro sensitivity
MEDIUM
A meaningful slice of profit comes from the treasury/investment portfolio, which is exposed to Selic, inflation (IPCA) and equity-market swings. The financial result has whipsawed from +121% (2T25) to −20% (1T26) YoY, repeatedly running below CDI. As rates eventually fall, the investment-income tailwind that supported recent quarters reverses. Timeline: rate-cycle dependent.

The valuation tension is the crux of the entire case. On a standalone basis the multiple looks undemanding: a high-ROE, double-digit grower trading near 8.5x trailing earnings and ~2.0x book, with a PEG below 1, would normally screen as cheap. The reason the multiple has not expanded further — and the reason three major houses cut to neutral in H1-2026 — is twofold. First, the re-rating already happened: the shares roughly doubled over the prior two-year window, pulling the P/E up from the high-single-digits toward the low-double-digits on forward estimates, so the gap between price and intrinsic quality has substantially closed. Second, the components of the recent earnings beat are partly non-repeatable — a benign auto-loss environment, an outsized financial result in mid-2025, and a one-off tax credit in 1T26 — which makes the Street cautious about extrapolating the growth rate that drives the low PEG. Against the historical 7–13x P/E band, PSSA3 today sits in the lower-middle: cheap enough that downside is cushioned, but not so cheap that a re-rating is the base case. The dividend yield near 6% provides a floor of total return, though it is below the best dedicated B3 dividend payers, so the stock is better understood as a quality-compounder held for earnings growth than as a pure income vehicle.

01 · PRICE DISLOCATION On absolute metrics, PSSA3 is not expensive: ~8.5x trailing earnings, ~2.0x book and a PEG below 1 for a business compounding profit at double digits with a ~25% ROE. That is a cheap multiple for the quality. The dislocation, however, is mostly behind it — the stock re-rated hard through 2024–25 (up roughly 70–80% over two years) and now sits in the upper half of its 52-week band (R$38.4–57.2), so the "obvious discount" trade has largely played out. What remains is a fairly priced compounder, not a mispriced one.

02 · FUNDAMENTAL QUALITY The franchise quality is genuinely high. Five straight quarters of double-digit recurring growth, an insurance combined ratio near 90%, structural ROE held above 20%, low leverage (D/E ~0.05), and a diversification handover where Health, Bank and Services now contribute roughly half of group profit. The broker network remains a durable distribution moat that direct insurers have not breached. This is among the better-run financials on the B3.

03 · PRIMARY RISK Three things temper enthusiasm. Auto — still the core — faces real price competition, with January-2026 premiums down 4.2%. Porto Bank's credit losses jumped 44% YoY and must stay within guidance in a stretched-household, high-Selic backdrop. And the financial result has been volatile and sub-CDI, a tailwind that reverses as rates eventually fall. None is thesis-breaking alone, but together they explain why BBI, UBS and BTG all migrated to neutral.

04 · NEAR-TERM CATALYSTS The next read is 2T26 on August 7 — historically the seasonal revenue peak, so a natural bar-setter. Watch credit-loss trajectory, any recovery in the financial result, auto pricing discipline, Health margin conversion, and reaffirmation of the ~R$3.7bn 2026 guidance. With consensus offering ~+13% to the average target (R$55) and a bull case toward R$67 against a bear floor near R$45, the setup is a balanced, quality-biased hold: the risk/reward is favourable for patient holders but lacks the margin of safety that would make it compelling at today's level.
