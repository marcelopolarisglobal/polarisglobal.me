1.4 Quarter-Defining Events

2Q25

Vulcabras opened the second quarter of fiscal 2025 with the first visible sign of an unusually large jump in profitability — net income up 152.9% YoY to R$353.3 million and EBITDA up 69% to R$296.4 million, against "only" 17.6% revenue growth. The disproportionate bottom-line move pre-dated the deferred-tax recognition that would dominate the following quarter's headlines and coincided with the early stages of a balance-sheet restructuring that management would later use to fund an extraordinary dividend program. Operationally, the quarter continued the company's now-familiar pattern: double-digit revenue growth led by sports footwear, with Olympikus's running franchise as the standout performer.

3Q25

The third quarter delivered the fastest revenue growth of the four-quarter window (+21.8% YoY, to R$955.7 million) and crossed R$1.1 billion in gross operating revenue for the first time in a single quarter — the company's 21st consecutive quarter of growth. The headline, however, was dominated by a one-off: the recognition of more than R$366 million in deferred tax assets pushed unadjusted net income to roughly R$547 million, nearly triple the prior-year figure, even as the cleaner recurring net income figure (R$163 million, +11.6% YoY) showed a much more modest — though still healthy — improvement. The quarter also marked an inflection in capital allocation: the board approved R$597.6 million in extraordinary dividends alongside a private capital increase of up to R$597.7 million, setting the stage for a sharp rise in financial leverage entering 2026.

4Q25

The fourth quarter closed out a record fiscal year with the company's first-ever R$1 billion net revenue quarter (+11.4% YoY), its 22nd consecutive quarter of growth. Recurring net income of R$158.8 million beat the Bloomberg-aggregated consensus of R$152 million by roughly 4.5%, and shares jumped as much as 6% intraday before settling up 2.7% on the day. Sell-side reaction was constructive but cautious: BTG Pactual called the print "encouraging," while XP Investimentos characterized results as "mixed," flagging a deceleration in revenue growth (from +22% in 3Q25 to +11% in 4Q25) against a backdrop of an unusually promotional retail environment with early Black Friday discounting. For the full year, Vulcabras posted record gross revenue of R$4.2 billion (+16.7%), net revenue of R$3.56 billion (+16.8%), recurring EBITDA of R$763.1 million (+13.0%, 21.4% margin) and recurring net income of R$572.9 million (+5.3%, 16.1% net margin) — alongside R$1.54 billion in total 2025 dividend distributions.

1Q26

The first quarter of 2026 extended the growth streak to 23 consecutive quarters, with record first-quarter net revenue of R$776.4 million (+10.7% YoY), driven once again by the sports footwear category (+11.3% to R$653.2 million, on volume growth of +10.5% to 4.8 million pairs). Operationally the quarter was solid — recurring EBITDA rose 11.8% to R$156.9 million with a slight margin improvement to 20.2%, and gross margin held near 40.4%. But the headline net income figures disappointed: reported net income fell 24.5% YoY to R$80.1 million (a 2.3% miss against the R$82 million Bloomberg consensus), and recurring net income fell 18.9% to R$86.1 million. The culprit was financial expenses — Vulcabras swung from a R$2.3 million net financial gain in 1Q25 to a R$27.8 million net financial loss in 1Q26, a direct consequence of the higher debt load taken on in late 2025 to fund working capital, capex and the extraordinary dividend program. Encouragingly, net debt fell 14.4% sequentially to R$658.9 million (0.7x EBITDA), and the company generated R$156 million of cash in the quarter, suggesting the deleveraging process announced by management is already underway. The quarter also included a R$6 million non-recurring charge related to the write-off of discontinued e-commerce software.

1.5 Recurring Earnings Patterns

  • 01. The revenue growth streak (23 consecutive quarters as of 1Q26) has been sustained primarily by price/mix improvements and premiumization rather than pure volume expansion — 1Q26 volume grew 6.8% while revenue grew 10.7%, and management has explicitly chosen to prioritize margin over volume in a promotional retail environment.
  • 02. Recurring EBITDA margin has compressed steadily across the four-quarter window — from an apparent ~33% in 2Q25 (likely inflated by one-offs) to 22.1% (3Q25), 21.9% (4Q25) and 20.2% (1Q26) — reflecting the cumulative impact of higher payroll costs from the company's industrial workforce expansion.
  • 03. Headline net income has become increasingly decoupled from recurring net income due to large non-operating items: a R$366M+ deferred tax credit in 3Q25 and a R$6M software write-off in 1Q26 are the most recent examples, requiring careful reconciliation each quarter.
  • 04. The net financial result has flipped from a net gain (R$2.3M in 1Q25) to a net loss (R$27.8M in 1Q26), directly tracking the rise in net debt from a low base to R$769.4M at year-end 2025, itself a consequence of the R$1.54bn in 2025 dividend distributions.
  • 05. Sports footwear — anchored by Olympikus's "Corre" running franchise and supplemented by the licensed Mizuno and Under Armour brands — remains the dominant growth engine in every quarter reviewed, consistently outpacing apparel and accessories.

2. Institutional Analysis

Business model, competitive positioning, risk, valuation and analyst coverage

2.1 Business Model & Revenue Architecture

Vulcabras is Brazil's largest manager of sporting-goods brands, operating a vertically integrated manufacturing model across two factories in Horizonte (Ceará) and Itapetinga (Bahia), with a distribution center in Extrema (Minas Gerais), a technology and development center in Parobé (Rio Grande do Sul), and an administrative headquarters in Jundiaí (São Paulo). The group employs more than 17,000 people and distributes through more than 19,000 points of sale in Brazil and across 15+ countries, with the strongest international presence in Latin America (Peru and Chile).

The company's brand architecture rests on three pillars: Olympikus, its own brand and Brazil's national leader in running footwear, particularly through its "Corre" performance line (including the world's first graphene-plate running shoe); Under Armour, operated under an exclusive Brazilian license signed in 2018; and Mizuno, whose Brazilian operations and license were acquired from Alpargatas in 2021 for R$32.5 million. Sports footwear is by far the largest category, representing roughly 84% of 1Q26 net revenue (R$653.2M of R$776.4M), with apparel and accessories making up the remainder.

2.3 Top 3 Risks

Brand License Concentration

High Severity · Medium-Term

Two of Vulcabras's three core brands — Under Armour and Mizuno — are operated under license agreements with foreign principals rather than owned outright. While both relationships currently appear constructive (Under Armour's global team reportedly approached Vulcabras about reshoring Asian production following US tariff changes, and the Mizuno license has been in place since 2021), license agreements are inherently time-limited and subject to renegotiation or termination outside the company's full control. Given that sports footwear represents roughly 84% of net revenue and that Olympikus alone would not replicate the combined scale of the current three-brand portfolio, any deterioration in either licensing relationship would represent a structural — not merely cyclical — risk to the long-term revenue base. Management's stated intent to pursue additional brand acquisitions (its only owned-brand addition since 2021) can be read as a partial hedge against this concentration.

Gross & EBITDA Margin Compression from Labor Costs

Medium Severity · Near-Term

Recurring EBITDA margin has declined in three consecutive quarters for which comparable data is available — from 22.1% (3Q25) to 21.9% (4Q25) to 20.2% (1Q26) — a pattern both BTG Pactual and XP Investimentos have attributed to the cumulative effect of workforce expansion at Vulcabras's domestic manufacturing plants undertaken during the 2024–2025 growth cycle. XP specifically characterized the gross margin pressure as "near an inflection point" as of the 4Q25 print, but 1Q26's 40.4% gross margin (down slightly from 41.4% in 4Q25) suggests the inflection has not yet fully materialized. If labor-cost normalization is delayed further into 2026, recurring earnings growth — already negative YoY in the two most recent quarters — could remain under pressure even as top-line growth persists.

Leverage-Driven Financial Expense Following the 2025 Capital Restructuring

Medium Severity · Near-Term to Medium-Term

The R$1.54 billion in dividends distributed during 2025 — funded in part by new debt — pushed net debt from a comparatively low base to R$769.4 million at year-end 2025 (0.9x EBITDA), and the resulting net financial result swung from a R$2.3 million gain in 1Q25 to a R$27.8 million loss in 1Q26. This single line item was the primary driver of the 18.9% YoY decline in recurring net income in 1Q26 despite an 11.8% increase in recurring EBITDA. While net debt already fell 14.4% sequentially in 1Q26 (to R$658.9 million, 0.7x EBITDA) and the company generated R$156 million of cash in the quarter — both encouraging signs — the pace of deleveraging and the trajectory of Brazilian benchmark interest rates will jointly determine how quickly this drag on recurring earnings dissipates.

VULC3 currently trades near its 52-week low of R$14.80, down sharply from levels above R$19.90 at the start of 2026 and from a recent high of R$22.14 in October 2025 — a roughly 26–33% decline that has compressed the TTM P/E to 4.15x and pushed the trailing dividend yield to an eye-catching 32.4%. Both of these headline figures are materially distorted by one-time items: the TTM earnings figure is inflated by 3Q25's R$366 million deferred tax recognition, and the trailing dividend yield reflects the R$1.54 billion extraordinary distribution program executed during 2025 that management has explicitly characterized as non-recurring. On a cleaner basis — BTG's cited ~8x 2026E P/E and the company's own ~8.2% 2026E dividend yield guidance — the valuation discount narrows considerably, though it remains low relative to the stock's own growth profile and the 40–52% upside implied by the R$22–24 price-target range referenced in Brazilian financial press.

On fundamentals, Vulcabras remains a high-quality operating story: 23 consecutive quarters of revenue growth, a vertically integrated manufacturing base that supports rapid product-mix shifts, and a three-brand portfolio (Olympikus, Under Armour, Mizuno) with genuine momentum in the sports footwear category, which grew 11.3% YoY in 1Q26 on the strength of Olympikus's premium "Corre" running line. The company crossed R$1 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time in 4Q25 and posted a record fiscal 2025 across nearly every headline metric. E-commerce, while still a minority of revenue at 15.3%, is growing at 25% annually and represents a structurally margin-accretive channel shift that is still in early stages.

The primary risk acknowledgement centers on the disconnect that has opened up between operational performance and recurring earnings over the two most recent quarters. Recurring net income fell 6.1% YoY in 4Q25 and 18.9% YoY in 1Q26 — not because the underlying business deteriorated (recurring EBITDA actually grew 14.8% and 11.8% respectively in those quarters), but because of a financial-expense swing driven by the leverage taken on to fund the 2025 dividend program, compounded by a steady erosion in EBITDA margin (from 22.1% to 20.2% across the four quarters reviewed) attributed by both BTG and XP to labor-cost pressure from the company's recent manufacturing workforce expansion. Until both of these trends visibly reverse — net debt continuing its sequential decline from the current 0.7x EBITDA, and gross margin stabilizing or recovering from 40.4% — recurring earnings growth is likely to remain under pressure even if top-line growth persists in the low double digits.

Near-term catalysts to monitor include the 2Q26 results release (expected early August 2026), where the trajectory of the net financial result and gross margin will be the key swing factors for the recurring earnings narrative; further clarity on 2026 dividend policy following the guided normalization to an ~8.2% yield; any concrete developments on the Under Armour manufacturing-reshoring discussion that emerged in response to 2025 US tariff changes, which could represent an incremental volume tailwind for the licensed Under Armour operation; and any signal of progress on management's stated interest in expanding the brand portfolio through M&A, which would represent the first such transaction since the 2021 Mizuno acquisition and could meaningfully extend the long-term growth runway beyond the current three-brand structure.
