1.4

Quarter-Defining Events

QuarterThe Story Behind the Numbers
1T26The quarter that flipped Klabin's 2025 narrative on its head. Volumes were genuinely strong — total shipments up 12% YoY, containerboard up 31% YoY, and cellulose long-fibre/fluff up 3% — yet the headline turned into a R$497mn net loss, reversing the prior year's R$446mn profit. The culprits were structural rather than operational: a stronger real compressing the ~60%-USD revenue base, a scheduled major maintenance shutdown at the Monte Alegre cartonboard/kraftliner complex that pushed cash costs to the top of guidance (~R$3.2–3.3k/ton), and a heavier financial result. Adjusted EBITDA of R$1.67bn missed the LSEG consensus of R$1.75bn by roughly 5%, and free cash flow turned negative on higher maintenance capex and working-capital consumption. JPMorgan had already downgraded the stock to Neutral three weeks before the print (Apr 23), citing a lack of near-term catalysts — a call this quarter's results did little to dispel, even as Santander and others held firm on Buy citing the valuation discount.
4T25A quarter engineered to look worse than it was. Net income collapsed 69% YoY to R$168mn, the kind of headline that dominates retail-investor commentary, but the adjusted EBITDA of R$1.83bn was essentially flat YoY and the FY2025 EBITDA of R$7.84bn (+7%) confirmed a genuinely resilient operating year. The drag came from scheduled maintenance outages at Ortigueira (PR) and Correia Pinto (SC) — both completed on time and on budget — plus the pulp segment's revenue falling 14% YoY on weaker fibre prices and a softer dollar. Net debt fell 22% YoY to R$25.9bn and leverage held at 3.3x, while the Board approved R$1.1bn in year-end dividends (~R$0.91/unit). Management's decision to discontinue its "incremental EBITDA" disclosure framework drew some market attention but did not change the underlying 2026 guidance of +4% revenue, +5% EBITDA and +8% net income growth.
3T25The cleanest quarter of the four — and the one that triggered a rating upgrade. Revenue grew 9% YoY to R$5.43bn and adjusted EBITDA jumped 17% to R$2.12bn, with margin expanding nearly 3 percentage points to 39%, "a little better than market consensus" per Nord Investimentos. Packaging revenue surged 20% YoY on export-driven demand from protein, fruit and tobacco shippers, while paper revenue rose 11% on a 10% volume increase in cartonboard. Net income still fell 34% YoY to R$478mn — financial expenses widened to R$670mn on FX-related debt revaluation — but net debt declined 12% YoY to R$26.1bn and leverage improved to 3.3x. The market reaction was visibly positive (units +2.2% intraday to R$18.38), and BB Investimentos upgraded the stock from Neutral to Buy with a new 2026 target of R$26.00, citing a 19% discount to historical EV/EBITDA.
2T25A quarter that split the room. On the surface, net income jumped 86% YoY to R$585mn, comfortably ahead of the ~R$486mn the market had pencilled in — yet the units fell roughly 1.2% on the day. The disconnect was about quality of earnings: adjusted EBITDA of R$2.04bn came in essentially flat YoY but missed Itaú BBA's R$2.15bn estimate by about 5%, as a 10% YoY rise in cash cost per tonne (to R$3,178, driven by caustic soda and other input costs, compounded by heavy rains disrupting forestry operations) ate into the margin gains from stronger paper and packaging pricing. Net debt rose 18% YoY to R$27.95bn and leverage widened to 3.7x — the high-water mark of the four-quarter window — even as the Board declared R$306mn in dividends. The long-fibre/fluff cellulose category, at 29% of volume but 42% of segment revenue, underscored how much of Klabin's pulp profitability is concentrated in its highest-spread products.
1.5

Recurring Earnings Patterns

#Structural Observation
01Earnings volatility vastly exceeds operating volatility. Across all four quarters, net income/loss swung from −R$497mn to +R$585mn (a R$1.08bn range) while adjusted EBITDA stayed within a R$1.67bn–R$2.12bn band — a sign that financial-result (FX/interest) noise dominates the bottom line far more than the operating business.
02Packaging is the consistent outperformer. Packaging revenue growth (+16% in 2T25, +20% in 3T25, +6% in 4T25, +6% in 1T26) has outpaced both paper and cellulose in every quarter, driven by food, protein, fruit and hygiene-sector export demand for corrugated boxes — the most defensive part of the franchise.
03Cellulose is the swing factor on both revenue and margin. Pulp segment revenue fell in 3 of 4 quarters (flat in 2T25, flat in 3T25, −14% in 4T25, −12% in 1T26) as fibre prices and a stronger real both worked against the export-heavy segment — yet long-fibre/fluff remains structurally the highest-margin product line.
04Deleveraging has been real but is plateauing. Net debt fell from R$27.95bn (2T25) to R$24.0bn (1T26), a genuine ~14% reduction, but the USD leverage ratio has been stuck at 3.3x for three consecutive quarters (3T25, 4T25, 1T26) — progress on absolute debt has not yet translated into ratio improvement, partly due to EBITDA softness.
05Scheduled maintenance shutdowns are a recurring near-term drag. Three of the four quarters referenced a major maintenance outage (Ortigueira/Correia Pinto in 4T25, Monte Alegre in 1T26) as a specific cost or volume headwind — these are calendar-predictable events but consistently compress the quarter in which they fall.

Section 2

Institutional Analysis

Klabin is Brazil's largest producer and exporter of packaging paper and one of the country's most vertically integrated forestry-to-packaging platforms, combining a globally relevant pulp business with a domestically-oriented paper and corrugated packaging franchise.

2.1

Business Model & Revenue Architecture

Klabin operates an integrated model spanning 400.4 thousand hectares of forestry land (253.4 thousand hectares of planted eucalyptus and pine, the remainder native/conservation areas), 24 industrial units (23 in Brazil, one in Argentina), and roughly 13,800 employees. FY2025 consolidated net revenue reached R$20.7bn (+5% YoY), with adjusted EBITDA of R$7.84bn (+7% YoY) and an EBITDA margin of 38%.

The model's core logic is that the cellulose business provides geographic and currency diversification and upside leverage to global pulp cycles, while paper and packaging — overwhelmingly tied to Brazil's food, beverage and agribusiness export complex — provide a structurally more stable earnings base. Recent capital allocation priorities have centred on the completion of the Puma II project (MP27 and MP28 paper machines) and the "Plateau Project," which increased Klabin's effective ownership in certain operating assets — a move that reduces minority-interest leakage on consolidated net income going forward but also explains part of the near-term EPS volatility analysts have flagged.

2.3

Top 3 Risks

1. Real Appreciation / FX Translation Drag

High · Ongoing
With roughly 60% of revenue denominated in or linked to USD, a stronger real mechanically compresses reported revenue and EBITDA even when underlying volumes and dollar-denominated prices are stable or rising. This was the single largest swing factor behind the 1T26 net loss and the 4T25 EBITDA margin compression, and BB Investimentos explicitly cited the "persistent retraction of the exchange rate" (real appreciation of roughly 10% year-to-date through May 2026) as a sector-wide headwind for Klabin and Suzano alike. Klabin's hedging policy covers an estimated 25–50% of natural FX exposure, which smooths but does not eliminate the translation effect on short-term free cash flow.

2. Elevated Leverage & Stalled Deleveraging

High · Multi-Year
Net debt/EBITDA on a USD basis has been pinned at 3.3x for three straight quarters (3T25, 4T25, 1T26), above the company's stated 2.5x–3.5x target band during non-investment-cycle periods. While absolute net debt has fallen from R$27.95bn to R$24.0bn over the period, EBITDA softness in 1T26 has offset the numerator improvement in ratio terms. With 2026 capex guidance raised to R$3.3bn (from R$2.9bn in 2025) before stepping down toward R$2.0–2.5bn in the long term, the deleveraging path depends heavily on a cellulose price recovery and FX stabilization materializing in 2026–2027 rather than being purely a function of capex discipline.

3. Pulp Price Volatility & China Demand Imbalance

Medium · Near-Term
The 1T26 results flagged a "persistent supply-demand imbalance" in the BSKP (bleached softwood kraft pulp) market in China, with overseas mills continuing normal operations and rising port inventories, while downstream paper industry profitability in China remained depressed — encouraging mills to substitute imported BSKP to manage input costs. This dynamic, combined with weaker fibre prices generally, drove cellulose segment revenue down in three of the last four quarters. A recovery is incipient (RISI Table 5 indices showed a modest +1% QoQ increase in long-fibre/fluff pricing into 1T26) but remains fragile and dependent on Chinese paper-industry demand recovery and the pace of the Spring Festival-related production restarts.

Risk/Reward
Asymmetric, Valuation-Supported

The price dislocation at KLBN11's current R$16.91 — near the bottom of its 52-week range of R$16.01–21.01 and well below the R$23.05–23.50 average analyst target — is largely a function of a single weak quarter (1T26's R$497mn net loss) layered onto a multi-quarter pattern of currency-driven earnings noise. The gap between the stock's current EV/EBITDA of roughly 6.0x and the sector's historical average near 7.0x, cited explicitly by both Genial and Citi as a discount, suggests the market is pricing in continuation of the adverse FX and cellulose-price trends seen through 1T26 rather than the gradual normalization both houses assume in their 2026 base cases.

On fundamentals, the underlying business remains of reasonable quality: FY2025 delivered 5% revenue growth, 7% EBITDA growth, a 10.5% ROIC, and a 9.2% adjusted FCF yield, while the packaging segment — the most defensive and fastest-growing part of the portfolio — has outperformed in every one of the last four quarters. The Puma II ramp-up (MP27/MP28) is operationally validated, and absolute net debt has fallen by roughly 14% since mid-2025. These are the elements that keep six of eight tracked sell-side houses at Buy.

The primary risk to this picture is that the leverage ratio has been flat at 3.3x (USD) for three consecutive quarters despite the absolute debt reduction — meaning EBITDA, not balance-sheet discipline, is now the binding constraint on the deleveraging story. Combined with a real that has appreciated roughly 10% year-to-date and a China cellulose market still working through an inventory overhang, the conditions that produced the 1T26 loss have not yet visibly reversed, which is the core of both BTG's and JPMorgan's Neutral theses and the reason PGS does not yet treat the valuation discount as a clear-cut entry signal.

Near-term catalysts to monitor center on the 2Q26 print (expected early August 2026): confirmation that Monte Alegre-related cash costs are normalizing back toward R$3,100/ton, any sequential movement in the USD/BRL leverage ratio off 3.3x, and continued sequential improvement in long-fibre/fluff pulp pricing per RISI Table 5 indices. A clear move on any two of these three would materially strengthen the bull case; continued stagnation would tend to validate the more cautious BTG/JPMorgan positioning and could see the average analyst target drift toward the R$22–23 area already staked out by the Neutral-rated houses.
