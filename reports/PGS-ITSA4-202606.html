Section 1 · Earnings Analysis

Last Four Reported Quarters

Anchor confirmed: 1T26 (Q1 2026) — results released to market after close on Monday, May 11, 2026 (PR Newswire, May 12). Verified against Itaúsa IR, Reuters and InfoMoney. Next release scheduled Aug 10–11, 2026.
Coverage sequence: 1T26 → 4T25 → 3T25 → 2T25 — continuous quarterly sequence, no gaps.

Itaúsa is an investment holding: it has no operating revenue of its own. Its income statement is the sum of equity-method results from its investees, dominated by Itaú Unibanco. The relevant "top line" for a holding is therefore recurring net income (lucro líquido recorrente) and recurring ROE, not sales. Across the four quarters, recurring net income has held a tight band around R$ 4.0–4.5 bn while ROE has stair-stepped from 18.4% to 20.1% — the clearest signal in the data.

1.4Quarter-defining events

QuarterThe story behind the numbers
2T25A record recurring quarter built almost entirely on Itaú (+12%), masking an 8% dip in the non-financial book (Dexco and Motiva soft). The real headline was capital allocation: management reaffirmed the multi-year deleveraging that had cut gross debt ~30% and lengthened maturities, and declared R$ 2.7 bn in JCP (R$ 0.245/share) — first-half shareholder returns up 47% YoY. The quarter cemented the "dividend machine with falling leverage" narrative.
3T25The softest of the four on the surface (+6% YoY, ROE slipping to 18.1%), and the only quarter where the non-financial portfolio actively detracted — Dexco's weak panels/results offset gains at Aegea, Motiva and Alpargatas. Yet it was still a nominal record, and it landed inside a powerful B3 rally (ITSA4 +47% YTD by then), so the market looked through the deceleration and treated the stock as an index-and-dividend vehicle rather than reacting to the print.
4T25The cleanest beat of the series: +21% YoY, ROE jumping to 19.6% from 16.6%, capping a record FY2025 of R$ 16.5 bn (+11%) in the holding's 50th-anniversary year. The non-financial book swung violently positive (+330% on a tiny base) and FY investee results reached R$ 17.6 bn (+12%). Management paired it with JCP of R$ 0.0957/share and a share-bonus, and a 76% payout — but the stock closed flat, the market already positioned.
1T26Peak ROE of the cycle (20.1%) and a strategically important quarter: non-financial investees surged +76% YoY, validating a decade-long diversification bet. NTS (+87% to R$ 157 mn) and a reversal at Aegea (+R$ 89 mn) led; Itaú still supplied >90% of recurring results (R$ 4.38 bn, +11%). The board approved a small ITSA4 buyback (up to 5 mn shares). The quarter sat under the shadow of April's Aegea accounting restatement — a R$ 700 mn equity hit booked just before, which kept sentiment cautious despite the strong print (stock −1.6% on the day).
Sources: Itaúsa management messages (Alfredo Setubal) via Money Times, ADVFN, Suno, Rio Times, Brazil Journal; Investidor10 (Aegea restatement).

1.5Recurring earnings patterns

#Structural observation across the four quarters
1Itaú is the result. In every quarter Itaú Unibanco supplied roughly 90–95% of recurring investee results. ITSA4 is, mechanically, a leveraged-by-discount proxy for ITUB4 plus a small non-financial option.
2ROE is grinding higher. 18.4% → 18.1% → 19.6% → 20.1%. The upward drift reflects both Itaú's own ~20%+ ROE and Itaúsa's lighter equity base after deleveraging and capital returns.
3The non-financial book is inflecting — but off a small base. After a negative 2T25, contributions turned positive and then surged (+330% in 4T25, +76% in 1T26). Impressive in percentage terms, still only ~5–10% of total recurring results in reais.
4Returns policy is the constant. Every quarter carried a JCP/dividend declaration; FY2025 payout reached 76% with ~R$ 11.9 bn distributed, anchoring an LTM yield near 9% — among the highest on B3.
5Holding cost discipline holds. Administrative expense stayed in a R$ 44–52 mn band — immaterial against ~R$ 4.4 bn of quarterly recurring income — so essentially all investee growth flows through to shareholders.
Synthesis of the four quarterly releases. Sources as cited in 1.3–1.4.

Section 2 · Institutional Analysis

Business, Moat, Risk & Valuation

2.1Business model & portfolio architecture

Itaúsa is Brazil's largest listed investment holding — frequently described as a Brazilian Berkshire in miniature — controlled by the Egydio de Souza Aranha (Setubal/Villela) and Moreira Salles families. It owns long-term equity stakes and lives off the dividends those stakes pay up. Itaú Unibanco is ~90% of portfolio value; everything else is a diversification option assembled since 2008.

2.3Top 3 risks

Single-asset concentration on Itaú UnibancoHigh
With ~90% of portfolio value and >90% of recurring results tied to one bank, ITSA4 is effectively a discounted ITUB4. Any deterioration in Brazilian credit quality, NII compression as Selic eventually falls, or a hit to Itaú's ROE flows straight through. The holding's diversification, while inflecting, is too small to cushion a bank shock.
Timeline: continuous · Severity: defines the entire thesis
Holding-discount persistence (re-rating may not come)Medium
The bull case needs the ~24–25% discount to SOTP to narrow. Discounts on holding companies are sticky and can stay wide for years regardless of fundamentals — they depend on liquidity, foreign flows, rates and sentiment toward banks. The fiscal catalyst (2027) is real but already partly known; the market has repeatedly been "indifferent" to the discount. Carry (the dividend) is paid while you wait, but the capital-gain leg is the uncertain one.
Timeline: open-ended; catalyst from Jan 2027 · Severity: caps upside if it never closes
Investee governance / accounting tail (Aegea)Medium
In April 2026, Aegea's conservative restatement cut Itaúsa's equity by R$ 700 mn (deemed <1% of R$ 89 bn equity, "immaterial") and Aegea's own 2024 equity was restated down ~R$ 5 bn; its IPO slipped to 2027. No cash was paid, but the episode exposes the opacity of unlisted investees and the limits of minority oversight, and adds a reputational/valuation overhang to the non-financial diversification story.
Timeline: near-term overhang into 2027 IPO · Severity: contained financially, larger reputationally

1 · Price dislocation. At ~R$ 12.5 against an average target near R$ 16.4–17.0 and a ~24–25% discount to sum-of-the-parts (roughly 300 bps wide of its own history), ITSA4 offers a recognisable dislocation: you buy LatAm's most profitable private bank at a discount, with a dividend yield ~21% above owning Itaú directly. The mispricing is structural, not a one-off — which is exactly why it has persisted, and why patience is the cost of entry.

2 · Fundamental quality. The four-quarter record is unusually clean for a holding: recurring net income rising R$ 4.04 bn → R$ 4.49 bn, recurring ROE climbing 18.4% → 20.1%, a record FY2025 (R$ 16.5 bn, +11%), a 76% payout, falling leverage, and a non-financial book finally inflecting (+76% in 1T26). Earnings quality and capital discipline are high; holding costs are immaterial.

3 · Primary risk. The same fact that makes ITSA4 attractive — ~90% concentration in Itaú — is the dominant risk, alongside a holding discount that may simply refuse to close and an Aegea governance/accounting tail that, while financially immaterial (R$ 700 mn, <1% of equity), dents the diversification narrative and pushes value crystallisation out to a 2027 IPO.

4 · Near-term catalysts. (i) The 2T26 print (~Aug 11, 2026) and whether recurring ROE holds 20%; (ii) Itaú's delivery against 2026 guidance; (iii) progress on the Aegea governance diagnosis; and (iv) the approach of the Jan 2027 fiscal-efficiency gain (LC 204/23, ~R$ 650 mn/yr) as the clearest mechanical trigger for discount compression. The conclusion is directional: a favourable risk/reward where the dividend pays you to wait for a re-rating that is probable but not guaranteed.
