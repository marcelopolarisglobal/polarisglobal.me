C

Reading the quarters

Q2 2026 · anchor · the cloud print

Prime Day moved into June this year, which loaded the June quarter with retail volume the prior year had booked in September. That shift explains a fair share of the North America line, and it also explains why September guidance reads soft on revenue while reading generously on operating income. The consequential number sits in cloud. AWS growth reaccelerated for a fourth straight quarter, cleared the sell side comfortably, and did so while segment margin climbed back toward its recent peak, which is the combination the market had stopped expecting after two years of deceleration. The headline earnings figure tells you almost nothing about operations. A very large non-operating revaluation of the Anthropic holding runs straight through net income and accounts for most of reported diluted EPS. Strip it out and the quarter still beats, by an ordinary margin rather than a spectacular one. Management framed capacity as demand constrained, pointing to gigawatt-scale silicon commitments from the two leading frontier labs. The extended-hours reaction favoured the cloud print over the guidance quibble.

Q1 2026 · record margin, vanishing cash

The March quarter was the operating high-water mark of the sequence. Company operating margin reached the highest level Amazon has reported, carried by North America retail efficiency and a cloud margin close to its own ceiling, and revenue cleared the top of management's guidance range. AWS reaccelerated to its fastest pace in fifteen quarters and the custom chips business crossed an annualised run rate management chose to headline. The reported earnings figure again carries a large Anthropic mark, and once that is removed the quarter lands broadly where analysts had modelled it. The market read it the same way. Shares moved less than a percentage point in the following session despite a revenue beat and a record margin, because the same release disclosed the largest quarterly capital outlay in the company's history and a trailing free cash flow figure that had all but disappeared. Management also flagged incremental Amazon Leo cost landing in the June quarter. The print was strong. What investors priced was the funding cost of the next two years.

Q4 2025 · the capital announcement

The December quarter beat on revenue and missed on earnings by a cent, and neither fact mattered. What moved the stock was a single sentence on the call: management intended to spend roughly two hundred billion dollars on capital expenditure during 2026, a step up of about half again on the year just closed. Shares fell more than five percent in the next session and kept sliding for weeks afterwards. The underlying quarter was decent. AWS growth accelerated to its fastest in thirteen quarters, advertising held its low-twenties pace, and reported operating margin was suppressed by three separate charges covering an Italian tax settlement, severance, and impairments tied largely to physical stores. Adjusted for those, margin expanded year over year. Free cash flow was still comfortably positive, and this is the last quarter in the sequence for which that holds. The release offered no free cash flow floor and no payback timeline for the capital programme, and the absence of both is what the sell side fixed on.

Q3 2025 · charges, and a record close

The September quarter is the cleanest illustration of how far reported operating income can sit from underlying performance. Two special charges, a settlement with the Federal Trade Commission over Prime enrolment practices and severance tied to planned role eliminations, pushed reported margin below ten percent. Adjusted, margin was more than two points higher and expanding. AWS reaccelerated to its fastest growth in eleven quarters, which was the number the market cared about, and the stock closed nearly ten percent higher the following session at a record. A substantial Anthropic gain sat in non-operating income that quarter as well; excluding both the gain and the charges, earnings landed almost exactly on consensus, which is the pattern that repeats through the whole sequence. Jassy told analysts the company would keep investing aggressively in capacity because the demand was visible. Depreciation had also begun to reflect a shortened useful-life assumption on certain servers, a quiet change with a long tail.

D

Patterns and watch list

Structural patterns across the four quarters

Reported earnings have decoupled from operations. In every quarter, an Anthropic revaluation or a special charge moved diluted EPS by an amount comparable to the whole consensus estimate.

AWS is the only segment whose growth rate is rising. Cloud growth increased in each successive quarter while the two retail segments moved sideways in a narrow band.

Consolidated margin is a cloud mix story more than a retail execution story. AWS carries a segment margin many times the International figure, so consolidated margin tracks cloud share of revenue closely.

The slide into cash deficit was gradual and explained by one line. Trailing free cash flow fell every quarter and turned negative at the anchor, driven entirely by property and equipment purchases.

Amazon runs three segments and one profit engine. Cloud contributes under a fifth of revenue and well over half of operating income, and the ratio has been widening. North America is a large, low-margin machine that has become steadily more efficient, and International is a thinner version of the same thing with a longer runway and a weaker margin base. The reporting structure hides the two businesses that matter most to the margin trajectory: advertising sits inside the retail segments and carries incremental economics closer to cloud than to logistics, and the custom silicon programme sits inside AWS and is the main lever on cloud gross margin.

The funding chain runs in one direction. Retail generates working capital float, because suppliers and third-party sellers are paid after customers pay, and that float plus cloud operating income funds the capital programme. It no longer funds all of it. The capital outlay in the anchor quarter alone exceeded the entire operating cash flow of that quarter, which is why free cash flow turned negative, and the shortfall has been bridged with a step change in long-term debt issuance rather than with retained cash. That is a deliberate choice, and it is the single most important fact about the current financial structure.

Operating leverage originates in three places, and the order matters. First, in cloud, where each incremental workload runs across infrastructure whose cost per unit of compute the company is actively lowering through its own chips. Second, in advertising, where inventory is already paid for by the retail business and the incremental cost of a sponsored placement is close to zero. Third, in fulfilment, where regional network redesign, robotics and delivery density lower cost per unit shipped without adding revenue. The first two show up in margin quickly. The third shows up slowly and is the reason North America margin has roughly doubled over three years. Against all of that sits the depreciation schedule on the current buildout, which has begun to land and will keep landing through the end of the decade.

Rate the mechanism, not the brand. Fulfilment density earns the top mark because it is arithmetic: the cost of the next delivery falls as the number of deliveries in a postcode rises, and no competitor has the volume to match the denominator. Walmart is the only firm close, and it is closer in groceries than in the long tail. The advertising pillar earns the same mark for a different reason. Amazon observes what people buy rather than inferring what they might want, and an advertiser paying for a placement adjacent to a completed purchase is buying a different product from an impression on a social feed. Neither pillar depends on being liked.

Cloud switching costs are strong and slowly weakening. Data gravity is real, committed-spend contracts are real, and the cost of rewriting an application estate around a different set of managed services remains prohibitive for most enterprises. What has changed is that model access has become portable. A customer choosing between frontier models can increasingly run the same model on more than one cloud, which moves the competitive question from lock-in to price and capacity availability. That is a worse fight to be in, and it is why the silicon programme matters beyond its own revenue line.

Custom silicon gets three stars rather than four because the advantage is contingent. It works if the chips stay a generation behind the merchant leader on performance while sitting far ahead on cost per unit of useful work, and if the two largest customers keep committing capacity at scale. Both conditions currently hold. Neither is structural. The Prime bundle earns three for a related reason: retention is high, but the bundle has been repeatedly widened with new content and services, which is a sign that the pricing power is being maintained by adding value rather than by charging for it.

Sequencing decides everything here, and the sequence has one gate. Silicon must work first. If Trainium delivers acceptable performance at a materially lower cost per unit of useful work than merchant accelerators, then the capital programme buys more sellable capacity per dollar, cloud gross margin holds while volume compounds, and the backlog converts at margins that justify the depreciation. If it does not, Amazon is buying the same accelerators as everyone else at the same prices, the buildout becomes a commodity capacity race, and the returns compress toward the cost of capital. Everything downstream depends on which of those is true, and the evidence so far points the right way without being conclusive.

Advertising is the second gate and a much easier one. It is already large, already growing faster than the company, and already the reason consolidated margin can expand while retail volumes grow at single digits. It needs no capital. Its practical constraint is inventory quality, which is why the push into live sport, Prime Video and third-party placements matters more than it looks.

Leo and Zoox are options, not drivers. Both consume cash now and will not contribute meaningfully inside the horizon of this report. They earn the emerging tag because each has cleared a real gate this year, orbit count for one and a federal exemption for the other, and because each attaches to distribution Amazon already owns. Rate them as call options funded from the retail float, and price them at close to nothing until the capital programme has visibly turned.

F

Risk register

Capital cycle outrunning monetisation

High

The mechanism is depreciation, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion. Assets bought during the buildout begin depreciating on installation, whereas the revenue they support arrives across the following years as capacity fills. Install faster than you contract and the expense lands ahead of the revenue, compressing consolidated margin even while cloud revenue grows. A shortened useful-life assumption on certain servers accelerates the effect. Timeline is immediate and runs through 2029. The observable is the relationship between two disclosures: the sequential change in quarterly capital outlay and the sequential change in AWS operating margin. Capex rising while cloud margin falls for two consecutive quarters is the signal the cycle has turned. A widening cash deficit funded by long-term debt is tolerable while backlog compounds, and stops being tolerable the moment backlog growth flattens.

Counterparty concentration in the AI backlog

High

Much of the contracted backlog that justifies the capital programme sits with a small number of frontier AI laboratories, two of which account for the gigawatt-scale silicon commitments management cites most often. Those counterparties are loss-making and dependent on continued external funding. A funding shock at either converts a committed multi-year contract into a renegotiation, leaving Amazon holding purpose-built capacity with a thinner secondary market than general-purpose compute. Amazon is also an equity holder in one of them, so the same shock would hit reported earnings twice, through the contract and through the revaluation flattering GAAP results. Timeline is 2027 to 2028, when the largest commitments ramp. The observable is disclosed backlog composition and any change of language on customer concentration in the annual filing.

Retail margin and policy exposure

Medium

North America margin expansion has come from cost per unit shipped falling faster than price competition erodes gross margin. Three things break that: tariff and trade policy raising landed cost on a heavily import-weighted assortment, a consumer trade-down shifting mix toward lower-ticket everyday essentials, and regulatory settlements of the kind that hit reported operating income twice in two years. Scrutiny of advertising and marketplace practices continues. The mechanism is margin rather than revenue: volume has proved resilient across cycles, and the gap between what a unit sells for and what it costs to move is thin enough that a few hundred basis points of input cost matters. Timeline is continuous. The observables are shipping cost growth against paid unit growth, and any new charge disclosed under other operating expense.

G

Scenarios

Bull

+38.0% to the bull target
  • Cloud growth holds near the anchor quarter pace through 2027 as backlog converts.
  • Custom silicon keeps cost per unit of useful work below merchant accelerators, protecting cloud margin as depreciation lands.
  • Free cash flow troughs within the next two quarters and inflects as installed capacity fills.

These hold only if the two frontier laboratory commitments ramp on schedule and no third-party capacity glut appears in 2027. They also require advertising to keep growing at roughly double the company rate, since that is what funds margin expansion while the depreciation schedule peaks. Under those conditions the market stops valuing Amazon on a cash flow figure distorted by a one-off buildout and returns to valuing it on operating earnings, which is the multiple re-rating that does most of the work in this case.

Bear

-19.3% to the bear case
  • Cloud growth decelerates back toward the mid twenties as capacity catch-up completes and demand normalises.
  • Depreciation lands ahead of revenue and compresses consolidated margin for four or more consecutive quarters.
  • One large AI counterparty renegotiates or defers, and backlog growth flattens.

These hold if the AI capacity cycle behaves like previous infrastructure cycles, where industry-wide capital commitments made at the same moment produce a supply overhang two to three years later. The Anthropic revaluations that have supported reported earnings would reverse in the same environment, removing the optical support from GAAP EPS at precisely the point operations weaken. The multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range and the shares retest the lower part of the 52-week band in the panel.

The dislocation is specific. Since February the market has priced Amazon on a free cash flow figure the company deliberately destroyed by pulling a decade of infrastructure spending into three years. The shares sit well below their 52-week high while the segment that drives the multiple has accelerated four quarters running. That gap exists because the capital programme carries no published payback schedule, so investors cannot time the return. Absence of a schedule is not absence of a return.

Underlying quality improved over the same period, and it becomes visible once reported figures are cleaned of investment marks and special charges. Consolidated margin expanded, retail cost per unit keeps falling, advertising compounds at roughly double the company rate with no capital attached, and cloud margin recovered from its trough while growth accelerated. This is a business earning more on each dollar of revenue than it did two years ago, while spending as though it were earning less.

The primary risk is the capital cycle set out in the risk register, and it deserves to be taken seriously rather than argued away. Depreciation is contractual and revenue is not. Counterparty concentration compounds it, because the funding shock that would slow backlog conversion would also reverse the Anthropic marks propping up reported earnings. The bear case is what previous infrastructure cycles have actually done.

Catalysts cluster around three disclosures. First, any revision to the 2026 capital figure and the framing around it, which may already have changed on the call held the evening this report was published. Second, the next backlog disclosure and whether its growth still outpaces revenue. Third, the free cash flow trough, which on the current trajectory should be visible within two or three quarters and would remove the objection doing most of the work in the bear case.
