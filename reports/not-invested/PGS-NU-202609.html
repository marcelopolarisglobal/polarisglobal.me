Block C

Reading the quarters

Q2 2026 · anchor · reported 13 August 2026

This was the print that broke the pattern. Cost of credit fell sequentially while the loan book kept compounding, so the risk-adjusted margin expanded by the widest step in the series. That single line moved the stock, not the revenue beat. Gross profit grew faster than revenue because provisioning eased, and credit's contribution to gross profit recovered to roughly two-fifths of the total after two quarters in which allowances had suppressed it. The efficiency ratio deteriorated sequentially, which management framed as timing: property and marketing spend absent from the first quarter landed in the second. Late-stage delinquency rose, attributed to seasonal migration of first-quarter early arrears, while the leading indicator improved. Incoming CFO Rob Livingston told analysts the current risk-adjusted margin looks sustainable, then explicitly declined to call it a floor. Susquehanna observed that even bullish investors had underwritten a materially lower level going in, which explains why the reaction was violent, well beyond what a routine beat produces. Mexico's full banking licence arrived in the same window.

Q1 2026 · reported 14 May 2026

Accounting and expectations came apart here. Revenue crossed a threshold the company had never reached, net income set a record, and the Street still scored the quarter as a miss, because consensus was calibrated to the IFRS revenue line, while the company headlines a higher managerial total. Credit loss allowances jumped sharply. Management gave three reasons: first-quarter seasonality, portfolio growth, and a mix tilting toward unsecured lending. Risk-adjusted margin gave back a full point as a result, the only sequential contraction in the series. The efficiency ratio printed at a record low, but CFO Lago conceded that most of that outperformance came from spend phased into later quarters, which removed the good news before anyone could bank it. Bank of America called it a second consecutive disappointing quarter and cut its Brazilian real profit estimates for 2026 and 2027. UBS trimmed its target while keeping a buy. The market's working hypothesis, unresolved at the time, was that the provision build signalled a credit cycle, and that seasonality was the wrong explanation. Mexico reached break-even.

Q4 2025 · reported 25 February 2026

The quarter that changed the measuring stick. Nu introduced its Managerial P&L, a reorganisation of IFRS line items built to explain value creation across a multi-product, multi-country platform, fully reconciled to IFRS and designed for external assurance. Net income and return on equity both set records. The shares fell close to a tenth the following session on volume more than triple the three-month average. Two things drove that. Management framed 2026 as a transition year and said plainly that AI infrastructure, return-to-office property costs and groundwork for a United States entry would push the efficiency ratio up. And the margin contained items that will not repeat: tax-related adjustments, plus a one-off levy to Prosofipo, the Mexican deposit protection fund that every Sofipo must fund. Absent that levy, management said risk-adjusted margin would have been broadly flat sequentially. Investors who had watched the stock multiply since 2022 were told to expect a spending year, and they sold the guidance rather than the quarter.

Q3 2025 · reported 13 November 2025

The last quarter under the old presentation, and the cleanest one to read. Revenue and net income both set records, return on equity reached the low thirties, and adjusted net income exceeded the IFRS figure by the usual share-based compensation gap. The adjusted result beat consensus by a double-digit margin, and revenue beat as well. The market response was muted anyway. Attention settled on the efficiency ratio, which on the basis then in use sat uncomfortably against a thesis built on structural cost advantage. Headline net interest margin compressed sequentially as the asset mix moved toward lower-yield secured lending and Brazilian funding costs rose, though risk-adjusted margin still expanded because cost of risk fell further than asset yield did. Lago walked analysts through the yield and funding mechanics at length under direct questioning from Morgan Stanley, which is usually a sign the disclosure was not carrying the argument on its own. That disclosure problem is what the managerial presentation was designed to fix.

Patterns and watch list

Structural patterns across the four quarters

  • Provisioning, not revenue, decides the quarter. Revenue rose in every period without exception, while gross profit, risk-adjusted margin and the post-print share reaction all tracked the direction of credit cost.
  • The reporting change severed consensus comparability. Two of the four prints were scored against a revenue definition different from the one the company leads with, and the resulting beat-or-miss labels stopped describing operating performance.
  • Efficiency gains are structural but the quarterly path is noisy. Marketing and property spend moved between the first and second quarters of 2026 in both directions, and management flagged the phasing both times, so the low print never stood on its own.
  • The market trades the guidance, not the result. Three of four quarters beat on adjusted earnings, yet two fell sharply because management used the call to describe a spending year.

Strategic architecture

Three engines run in parallel, and they do not depend on each other in the way a conventional bank's segments do. Credit cards supply the entry product and the behavioural data. Unsecured and secured lending supply yield. Fees and float supply earnings that carry no credit cost at all. What makes the structure work is the order in which a customer passes through them. Nu acquires almost entirely through referral and brand at a cost per customer that traditional banks cannot approach, monetises first through the card, then through deposits it holds cheaply, then through lending priced off data the first two products generated. Each stage funds the next without external capital.

The fastest-growing line last year was float income on assets held at amortised cost, which is the deposit franchise doing its work. Deposits are gathered at a fraction of the interbank rate, and the gap between that funding cost and the yield on the credit book is where the economics live. Unsecured lending grew fastest among the credit lines, which is also where the risk sits, and the second-quarter margin expansion shows management pushing that mix on purpose.

Operating leverage originates in the cost line, not the revenue line. Monthly cost to serve an active customer sits below a dollar, and it has stayed there while revenue per active customer has roughly doubled over three years. That single ratio explains the efficiency ratio and the return on equity together. The risk is that the leverage is now partly spent: management has committed the incremental margin to AI infrastructure, Mexican banking operations and a United States build, so the ratio improves more slowly from here even as the underlying cost advantage holds.

Rate the mechanism, not the brand. The cost advantage earns the top mark because it is arithmetic rather than reputation: no branch network, no legacy core system, and a support function where AI agents now handle the majority of Brazilian conversations at or above human quality. An incumbent cannot match that without dismantling assets it already owns, and a new entrant cannot match the scale over which the cost is spread. That is the pillar most likely to survive a downturn intact.

The data pillar is stronger than it looks from outside and weaker than the company's framing implies. A decade of transaction history across more than a hundred million customers is a genuine input advantage, and NuFormer is now in production across three portfolios with two more in testing. The evidence management offers is that delinquency among customers who bank primarily with Nu runs at roughly half the portfolio average. That is a real finding, though it also describes selection: primary-relationship customers would be better credits at any lender. The underwriting edge is demonstrated in Brazil across income bands; it is asserted, not yet demonstrated, in Mexico and Colombia, and management told analysts it expects to need between one and three years of local data before it trusts United States models.

The deposit franchise is the pillar under most pressure, because it is the one competitors attack directly. Consolidated cost of deposits has improved against interbank rates, which shows the franchise holding. Brand and referral acquisition carry the lowest rating among the strong pillars for a simple reason: the mechanism has never been tested outside Latin America, and the United States launch prices against incumbents with distribution Nu does not have.

Sequencing matters more than the list. Brazilian monetisation has to work first, because it funds everything else from internal cash, with no call on capital markets. Nu already serves most of the Brazilian mass market and holds a small single-digit share of a domestic profit pool worth more than a hundred billion dollars a year, so the growth is no longer about adding customers. It is about revenue per customer, which is why Croma and Ultravioleta matter: they move the company into the segments where incumbent banks still earn most of their profit.

Mexico is second in sequence and first in evidence. The licence converts a credit-first fintech into a full institution able to gather payroll deposits, and the cohort data management presented shows Mexican customers monetising earlier than Brazilian customers did at comparable penetration. If Mexico compounds the way Brazil did, it removes the single-country concentration that the current multiple partly reflects.

The United States sits last and should be read that way. Management said profitability there will take a number of years, against eight in Brazil, and capped the investment inside the guided efficiency envelope. The launch runs through a partner bank while the charter clears the Federal Reserve and the FDIC, which means shared compliance and shared revenue in the interim. Nothing in the next two years depends on it working. Everything in the next decade might.

Block F

Risks

Brazilian unsecured credit cycle

High

The mechanism is straightforward. Nu has deliberately expanded into higher-risk, higher-return segments, and unsecured lending is the fastest-growing line in the book. That mix raises yield immediately and raises losses with a lag of two to four quarters. The first quarter of 2026 showed what the lag looks like when it arrives: allowances built sharply, risk-adjusted margin contracted, and the market treated a record revenue quarter as a disappointment. Timeline is continuous, with no single trigger date and the next reading in November. The observable that would signal it materialising is late-stage delinquency rising at the same time as the leading early-arrears indicator, in a quarter where seasonality argues for the opposite. Management has said the current risk-adjusted margin should not be treated as a floor, which is the clearest statement available that it does not consider the cycle settled. Brazilian household leverage and the Selic path sit underneath all of it.

Rate path and currency translation

Medium

Nu reports in dollars, earns in reais, pesos and Colombian pesos, and is listed in New York. Two separate effects follow. Falling Brazilian policy rates compress asset yields on the credit book faster than they compress deposit costs, because deposits are already priced at a discount to interbank rates and have less room to fall. Selic cuts through 2026 have already begun that squeeze. Separately, dollar translation moves reported revenue and earnings independently of operating performance, which is why the company presents growth on an FX-neutral basis and why headline and constant-currency growth rates have diverged by more than ten points in recent quarters. The timeline runs with the Brazilian easing cycle. The observable is net interest margin compressing while risk-adjusted margin holds, which would isolate the rate effect from the credit effect described above.

Four-jurisdiction expansion and capital allocation

Medium

Nu is simultaneously integrating a Brazilian banking licence through an acquisition, operating a newly licensed Mexican bank, scaling Colombia, and launching in the United States through a partner-bank arrangement while its own charter clears two federal regulators. Each carries capital, compliance and management-attention cost, and the efficiency ratio absorbs all of them at once. The commitment is to keep United States investment inside a defined band of the consolidated ratio, which is a constraint management set for itself, with no external enforcement behind it. Timeline runs through 2027, when the charter is expected to convert. The observable is the efficiency ratio breaching full-year guidance without a corresponding revenue explanation, or a shift in language at the December investor day from disciplined containment toward accelerated spending. Acquisition is explicitly on the table for United States growth, which would change the capital picture materially.

Scenarios

Bull case

+53.1% to the high target
  • Second-quarter risk-adjusted margin proves a plateau rather than a peak.
  • Mexico monetises on the Brazilian curve under its new licence.
  • Efficiency lands inside guidance despite United States spending.

Holding requires cost of credit to stay near the level reached in the second quarter through at least two more prints, which in turn requires the unsecured mix shift to be a deliberate pricing decision, with risk appetite held where management says it sits. Mexican cohorts must keep monetising earlier than Brazilian cohorts did at equivalent penetration, and the payroll products the licence permits must gather deposits at Brazilian-style funding costs. On those conditions the multiple re-rates toward its own recent history, with no expansion beyond it, and the earnings base does the rest of the work.

Bear case

-33.4% to the low target
  • The first-quarter provision build was a cycle signal, not seasonality.
  • Brazilian rate cuts compress yields faster than funding costs fall.
  • Expansion spending breaks the efficiency guidance.

Holding requires late-stage delinquency to keep climbing into a seasonally favourable quarter, which would confirm that the higher-risk expansion was priced too thin. Add a faster Selic easing path and the spread narrows from both ends at once. The third condition is the one management controls least: four simultaneous jurisdictional builds, an acquisition in Brazil, a possible acquisition in the United States, and a charter conversion, all landing while the credit line deteriorates. Bank of America has held a sell rating through the recent sequence, and the low target reflects that combination, with no single element of it sufficient alone.

Price dislocation. The shares have fallen over the past year while earnings have grown at the fastest rate in the company's public history, and the trailing multiple has compressed below every fiscal year-end level since 2023. The dislocation is explainable rather than mysterious. Two consecutive quarters were read as credit deterioration, the reporting change severed the consensus comparison, and management used both calls to describe a spending year. The second quarter began to resolve the first of those three, and the market moved sharply on it. The other two remain open.

Fundamental quality. Return on equity in the low thirties, a cost to serve that no incumbent can match, a deposit franchise funded well below interbank rates, and a customer base that keeps monetising better each quarter. The cost advantage described in the moat table is arithmetic, which is why it carries the top rating. The quality question is not whether the model works. It is whether the company can run four jurisdictional builds at once without spending the operating leverage that makes the model work.

Primary risk. The Brazilian unsecured credit cycle, rated High in the risk register, dominates everything else. Every other variable in this report is second-order against it. If late-stage delinquency keeps rising into a seasonally favourable quarter, the second-quarter margin expansion reads as a seasonal artefact and the turn never happened, and the bear conditions begin to assemble. Management's refusal to describe the current level as a floor is the honest framing and should be taken at face value.

Near-term catalysts. Third-quarter results on 12 November 2026 are the first clean test of whether credit cost has genuinely turned. The first investor day, on 8 December 2026 in New York, is where long-term targets get set and where the spending language will either tighten or loosen. Underneath both sits the United States launch of 10 September 2026, which contributes nothing to earnings for years but reframes what the business is: a partner-bank structure with a Mastercard credit product, fee-free remittance corridors into Brazil, Mexico and Colombia, and a multi-currency account spanning more than thirty-five countries, pending a charter conversion expected next year.
