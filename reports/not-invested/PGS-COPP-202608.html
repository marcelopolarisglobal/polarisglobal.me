Block C

Reading the quarters

What produced each number, and whether the market read the print correctly.

Q2 2026 · anchor quarter

The quarter was won in April and May and handed back in June. Copper cleared fourteen thousand dollars a tonne on the London exchange for the first time during May and the producer complex re-rated with it. What broke the run was company-specific. Freeport's first-quarter release on 23 April cut the expected second-half operating rate at Grasberg from roughly eighty-five per cent of capacity to about sixty-five, and the shares fell more than thirteen per cent in a single session. Because Freeport is a quarter of this fund, that print set the tone. June then produced two sessions worse than six per cent as the metal corrected. The gap to the benchmark was the widest of the four quarters shown, consistent with fee drag plus the friction of holding Australian, Indonesian and Peruvian lines whose closes do not align with the index calculation window. The market read the revision correctly: deferred volume is still volume, but the discount rate on a 2027 recovery is not the one applied to 2026.

Q1 2026

The most instructive quarter of the four. Copper set its all-time London high in late January, jumping around eleven per cent intraday before closing up roughly four, and the fund finished the quarter almost exactly where it started. Three things explain the disconnect. The equities had already discounted a supply shock through the second half of 2025, so the metal was catching up to the miners rather than leading them. Freeport carried a live execution overhang that no copper price could offset. And gold, which supplies a meaningful by-product credit to the largest holding, pulled back hard over the same window. Sell-side positioning turned defensive: Goldman Sachs put fair value near eleven and a half thousand dollars a tonne against a much higher spot price and framed a definitive tariff ruling as the correction catalyst. The equity market did not believe the spot print was durable, and on the evidence of the following quarter it was right to hesitate.

Q4 2025

The strongest quarter in the sequence, and the one that built most of the trailing one-year figure in the panel. The September mud rush at Grasberg stopped being an accident report and became a supply model revision. Goldman Sachs cut its global mine supply forecast and estimated roughly half a million tonnes of lost output across two years from that single asset; Benchmark Mineral Intelligence raised its 2026 deficit estimate more than fivefold. Three-month London forwards set an all-time high in October, the first time the forward curve had confirmed what the American futures contract had been signalling since mid-year. The miners re-rated on the arithmetic rather than on sentiment: at a fixed cash cost, every dollar of price increase falls to gross profit. The fund paid its annual distribution in December, which is why the quarter's total return runs ahead of the price move. The reaction was proportionate to the news, which is rare enough in this sector to note.

Q3 2025

Two supply events inside eight weeks. The seismic collapse at El Teniente removed a large Chilean operation from the market and sent the metal up around twelve per cent to just above nine thousand seven hundred dollars a tonne. Then on 8 September the Grasberg block cave flooded, and futures ran to roughly ten and a half thousand dollars, a fifteen-month high. Between the two, the American Section 232 action of mid-2025 landed and left refined metal outside the tariff perimeter, which collapsed the American futures premium and pushed the pricing signal back onto London. For a fund whose largest holding operates the mine that flooded, the quarter should have been ugly. It was not, because the supply loss lifted the price received by every other producer in the portfolio by more than it cost the one that suffered the loss. That asymmetry is the single most useful thing to understand about this vehicle.

The allocation is a barbell that pretends to be a market-cap index. At one end sit ten producers that together carry roughly three quarters of the money, and inside that group a single American name carries a quarter on its own. At the other end sit sixty-five positions, most of them under half a per cent, that own drill results rather than mines. Between the two sits a physical metal sleeve, added to the fund in June 2025, which is the only line in the portfolio that cannot miss a production guide.

The economics of the producer end are simple and violent. Antofagasta's first half showed the shape: revenue up around eighteen per cent to four and a half billion dollars, earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation up twenty-seven per cent to two and eight, and a margin above sixty-three per cent, all delivered while copper output fell nine and a half per cent year on year. Volume did not drive that result. Realised price did, at six dollars nineteen a pound against a net cash cost of one twenty-two. Teck said the same from a different asset base: copper segment gross profit before depreciation more than doubled on record quarterly sales at Quebrada Blanca.

Operating leverage originates entirely in the spread between realised price and unit cash cost, which is why the return distribution is skewed rather than symmetric. Freeport quantified it on its own call: ten cents a pound of copper is worth roughly four hundred million dollars of annual earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation at 2027 and 2028 volumes. No holding funds another, because there are no internal capital transfers. The structure pools price exposure while diversifying operational failure risk. Geographically the money sits mainly in Canadian and American listings, with Chile, Australia and Poland behind, so the fund is long Andean geology through North American paper.

Rate the mechanism, not the brand. Two pillars are genuinely hard to copy. The physical sleeve works because Sprott already operates a closed-end trust holding warehouse copper, and a rival issuer would need to build that vehicle first and then satisfy its regulator about owning it inside a fund. The second is duller and more valuable: the plumbing to hold seventy-nine lines across Toronto, London, Sydney, Warsaw, Hong Kong, Jakarta, Lima, Manila and Stockholm, settle them, and price them into a daily net asset value. A competitor can copy the idea in an afternoon and the infrastructure in about eighteen months.

The other three are ordinary. Index co-development sounds proprietary and is mostly a licensing arrangement; Nasdaq will sell a copper index to anyone. The purity screen is a threshold test on revenue or assets, so it admits and expels constituents on accounting definitions rather than economic reality, and the Anglo Teck combination will test exactly that. Distribution is the weakest of the five, because the fee sits above the passive average and the case for paying it rests on the exposure being unavailable elsewhere.

Durability therefore depends on whether pure-play copper stays a distinct category or gets absorbed into broader critical-materials products. On current evidence the category is holding. The honest read is a moat that protects the shape of the exposure rather than the level of the fee.

Sequencing matters more than the list. The supply deficit has to hold first, because everything else is a modifier on it. Global mine production fell in the opening five months of 2026 and refined output growth was forecast at well under one per cent for the year, which is the condition that lets the second driver matter at all. If mine supply recovers faster than expected, data centre demand becomes an interesting statistic rather than a price driver.

The tariff ruling sits second and is the only genuinely binary item. A decision either way ends the American stockpiling trade that has kept London inventories thin, and its direction determines whether the inventory release is orderly or violent. Until it lands, the London price contains a policy premium of unknown size, and so does every producer equity here.

The Anglo Teck combination is third and is specific to this fund rather than to copper. It resolves inside the tariff window and forces the index to judge whether the successor still clears the purity screen. Management describes the combined group as offering more than seventy per cent copper exposure, which suggests it does, at a weight and a listing that will differ from what the fund holds today. The developer tail is last and is the only driver that can still work if the first three disappoint, because takeover premiums in a scarce-asset market price off replacement cost rather than spot.

Block F

Risk register

Single-name concentration in Freeport-McMoRan

High

The mechanism is arithmetic. With a quarter of the money in one name, a fifteen per cent single-day move in Freeport moves the fund by close to four per cent before anything else happens, and the April 2026 session proved the transmission works exactly that way. The weight is not the result of a decision: it drifts upward between rebalances whenever Freeport outperforms, and the semi-annual schedule has meant six-month gaps during which nothing corrects it. Timeline is continuous, with the acute window running through the second half of 2026 while the block cave ramps toward the guided share of capacity. The observable is the holdings file: if Freeport's weight is still climbing after the September rebalance, the methodology permits concentration rather than controlling it. A second observable is Indonesian, since the Grasberg operating rights extension remains conditional and the disclosed post-2042 ownership step-down puts the largest holding's best asset inside a negotiated arrangement.

Policy reflexivity in the copper price

High

A material part of the current price is a tariff expectation rather than a physical shortage. American importers have been pulling metal across the Atlantic ahead of a Section 232 ruling that missed its June 2026 deadline, which drained London warehouses, widened the cash to three-month spread to levels last seen in the 2021 squeeze, and pushed the metal to record highs. That is a positioning trade wearing the costume of a supply deficit. Goldman Sachs has put fundamental fair value materially below spot and named the tariff decision as the correction catalyst. Timeline is the next two to four quarters. The observable is the spread and the warehouse balance together: a rebuild in London stocks alongside a collapsing backwardation would signal the arbitrage unwinding, and the mid-August session in which one trading house delivered twenty thousand tonnes showed how quickly that starts.

Jurisdictional and index-mechanics risk

Medium

The portfolio's tonnes sit in Indonesia, Chile, Peru, Congo, Zambia, Panama and Poland, and its listings sit somewhere else again. Each has an independent mechanism for interrupting production or cash flow: permitting, royalty change, export ban, seismic event, state ownership. The Congolese export ban is currently doing more to tighten the market than any producer decision. The index adds a second layer, since a purity screen decides which of those exposures the fund actually owns, and the Anglo Teck completion forces a decision on roughly a tenth of the portfolio. Timeline runs from September 2026 to March 2027. The observable is the rebalance file itself: a reconstitution that removes or materially reduces the successor entity would change the fund's risk profile without any market event occurring.

Scenarios

Constructive

+45.7% to the bull target of $60.00
  • The deficit is physical rather than positional, and survives the tariff ruling intact
  • Grasberg holds its ramp schedule and Freeport's execution discount closes
  • Data centre and grid demand shows up in refined consumption data rather than in producer commentary

This requires the metal to hold current levels through a policy resolution, which is the specific thing it has not yet been tested against. The supporting evidence is real: mine output fell in the opening months of the year, two tier-one assets stay impaired into 2028, and the Congolese export ban is starving Chinese smelters of feed. If those hold, producer margins of the kind Antofagasta reported in the first half become the sector norm rather than a peak, and the developer tail re-rates on takeover logic. The path runs through the September rebalance without a concentration accident and through the Anglo Teck completion without an index expulsion.

Adverse

-35.6% to the bear case of $26.50
  • The tariff decision releases American inventory and the London squeeze unwinds
  • Grasberg and Kamoa recover faster than the 2028 assumption embedded in supply models
  • Chinese buying stalls above current price levels, as it did in 2024

The bear case does not need a recession. It needs the price to revert toward the fundamental level Goldman Sachs has identified, roughly a fifth below spot, at which point the geared relationship described in Block D works in reverse and takes the fund down by considerably more than the metal. The developer tail is the accelerant here: those positions have no cash flow, thin liquidity and financing needs, and they de-rate first. The observable trigger is a warehouse rebuild in London, which began in mid-August and would need to persist for several weeks to confirm.

Balanced

Price dislocation. There is none to exploit. The fund sits modestly below its record after a trailing year that roughly doubled it, and the discount to net asset value is inside the bid-ask spread. The live question is whether the holdings are mispriced against a copper price carrying an unquantified policy premium. Nobody can size that premium until the Section 232 ruling lands, so taking a view on the fund today means taking a view on that ruling, intended or not.

Fundamental quality. The underlying businesses are in the best shape of the cycle. Antofagasta produced a first-half margin at the top of the pure-play group while losing volume, the clearest demonstration that price is doing the work. Teck's Chilean asset delivered record sales into a record price. Freeport is the exception and also the largest weight, running an impaired flagship while its American operations perform. The deficit case rests on evidence rather than projection: production fell, two tier-one mines stay impaired into 2028, and a producing nation has banned concentrate exports.

Primary risk. Concentration and policy reflexivity compound rather than offset. If the tariff arbitrage unwinds while Freeport's ramp slips, the fund takes both hits in the same quarter and the developer tail amplifies the second. The design assumes operational failure at one holding is offset by higher prices for the rest, which worked beautifully in the third quarter of 2025. It works while physical scarcity sets the price. It fails when a customs decision does.

Near-term catalysts. Four, all inside two quarters: the Section 232 ruling; Freeport's third-quarter Grasberg tonnage, which validates or breaks the guided second-half rate; the September rebalance, the first test of concentration control; and the Chinese and South Korean clearances for Anglo Teck. A holder with a multi-year horizon and tolerance for a forty per cent drawdown has a defensible entry. Anyone looking for the copper trade at an attractive price has arrived about a year late.
