1.4 Quarter-Defining Events

QuarterThe Story Behind the Numbers
Q2 2025The quarter that confirmed the succession era had begun. Reported days after Buffett's May 3 announcement that he would step down as CEO at year-end, the print showed Berkshire's first headline operating-earnings decline in several quarters (-3.8%), driven by a 12% drop in insurance underwriting and a $3.8B writedown on the long-troubled Kraft Heinz stake. Management used the release to issue one of its starkest tariff warnings yet, while the cash pile dipped slightly to $344.1B as Berkshire continued selling equities for an eleventh straight quarter. The stock, already down more than 10% from its post-announcement high, closed at $472.84 the day before the print.
Q3 2025A sharp rebound that masked a structural transition. Operating earnings jumped 34% YoY to $13.49B, powered by an insurance underwriting recovery (more than tripling to $2.37B) after a catastrophe-heavy stretch, plus a currency tailwind on non-USD debt. The cash hoard hit a fresh record of $381.7B with zero buybacks through nine months — even as the stock pulled back double digits from its highs, a move analysts attributed to the fading "Buffett premium" ahead of the CEO handover. The quarter also confirmed the $9.7B OxyChem acquisition announcement, Berkshire's first sizeable deal in years.
Q4 2025Greg Abel's inheritance, quantified. The annual report (Berkshire's first under Abel's authored shareholder letter) revealed a full-year operating earnings decline of roughly 6% to $44.49B, with insurance underwriting profit down nearly 20% to $7.26B and the Insurance & Other segment's operating earnings falling 12.9% for the year. Per-share operating earnings of $4.73 missed consensus by 8.9%, and the stock fell almost 5% in the next session — Berkshire's worst single-day reaction in years. Full-year net earnings of $66.97B (down from $89B) and a 10% share-price gain in 2025 (versus the S&P 500's 16.4%) framed a year of underperformance heading into the leadership change.
Q1 2026The first quarter with Abel fully at the controls — and the first signs of a different playbook. Operating earnings rose 18% YoY to $11.35B (still missing the $11.56B FactSet estimate), with Geico underwriting earnings down 34% even as overall insurance underwriting improved to $2.3B pre-tax on the absence of large catastrophes. The headline event was capital allocation: Berkshire closed the $9.5B OxyChem acquisition on January 2, resumed buybacks ($234M, the first since May 2024) and — as later confirmed via 13F — tripled its Alphabet stake to roughly $22.5B, making it the fifth-largest equity position. Cash nonetheless grew to a record $397.4B, underscoring that deployment remains incremental relative to the size of the hoard. PacifiCorp wildfire liabilities (cumulative ~$2.9B, ~$577M unpaid) remained a live overhang following an adverse Oregon appellate ruling.

1.5 Recurring Earnings Patterns

1. Operating earnings remain volatile quarter-to-quarter despite the "steady conglomerate" narrative. The four-quarter sequence ranges from a 27.7% per-share decline (Q4 2025) to a 33.6% increase (Q3 2025), driven largely by insurance underwriting swings tied to catastrophe timing rather than any structural change in the underlying businesses.

2. The cash hoard has grown in three of the last four quarters, breaking $397B by Q1 2026. Even with the OxyChem deal, GOOGL stake build, and resumed buybacks, net cash generation continues to outpace deployment — Berkshire has now been a net seller of equities for 14 consecutive quarters as of Q1 2026.

3. Insurance underwriting is the single largest swing factor in quarterly results. From a 12% decline (Q2 2025) to a >200% surge (Q3 2025) to a near-20% full-year decline (FY2025) to a partial Q1 2026 recovery offset by a 34% Geico drop, underwriting volatility — not the railroad, energy, or manufacturing operations — explains most of the headline beat/miss outcomes.

4. Capital allocation signals are accelerating under Abel. Four consecutive quarters of inaction (no buybacks, continued net equity sales) gave way in Q1 2026 to the first buyback in nearly two years, the largest acquisition since 2022 (OxyChem), and a tripling of the Alphabet position — a meaningfully different cadence than the prior several years.

5. Stock price reaction has been increasingly tied to "Buffett premium" sentiment rather than fundamentals alone. The Q3 2025 beat produced only a modest +2.1% reaction amid broader share weakness tied to the succession announcement, while the Q4 2025 miss triggered a outsized -4.9% drop — evidence that the market is now pricing execution risk around the leadership transition as much as the reported numbers themselves.

2. Institutional Analysis

2.1 Business Model & Revenue Architecture

Berkshire Hathaway is a diversified holding company combining (i) a large insurance and reinsurance operation (GEICO, Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group, Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group) that generates investable float at near-zero cost, (ii) a portfolio of large, wholly-owned operating businesses spanning rail (BNSF), regulated utilities and energy (BHE), manufacturing, wholesale distribution (McLane), fuel/travel retail (Pilot), and a broad services and retailing segment, and (iii) a roughly $280–290B marketable equity securities portfolio managed historically by Buffett and now by Greg Abel alongside long-time investment managers Todd Combs and Ted Weschler. The float-funded balance sheet — combined with a culture of decentralized operating management and centralized capital allocation — is the structural core of the business model.

2.3 Top 3 Risks

1. Post-Buffett Succession & "Oracle Premium" Erosion

High Severity

Greg Abel formally became CEO on January 1, 2026, ending six decades of Buffett leadership. While Abel built BHE into the largest U.S. wind producer and has decades of internal experience, CFRA analyst Cathy Seifert has publicly noted that he has "never professionally managed investment funds" — precisely the capital-allocation role most associated with Berkshire's historical outperformance. The stock has underperformed the S&P 500 by roughly 22 percentage points since Buffett's May 2025 succession announcement, and the Q4 2025 earnings miss triggered the steepest single-day decline in years, evidence that the market is actively re-rating the "Buffett premium" embedded in Berkshire's historical book-value multiple.

Timeline: Ongoing — re-rating risk persists through Abel's first 12-24 months as CEO, with the August 2026 and subsequent earnings reports serving as key trust-building (or trust-eroding) events.

2. Cash Deployment / Opportunity-Cost Drag

High Severity

The $397.4B cash and short-term Treasury position — a record — represents capital earning Treasury-bill yields rather than Berkshire's historical equity-like returns. Berkshire has been a net seller of equities for 14 consecutive quarters, and aside from the $9.5B OxyChem deal and the GOOGL stake build, deployment has remained modest relative to the size of the hoard. With the "Buffett Indicator" (total U.S. market cap to GDP) reported at 226.8% as of April 2026 — well above its 88% historical average — management's stated rationale is to wait for valuations to normalize. The risk is that this discipline, applied for too long, compounds an opportunity cost that the market increasingly penalizes via a lower book-value multiple.

Timeline: Medium-term (1-3 years) — resolution depends on either a market correction creating deployment opportunities or Abel demonstrating willingness to act at current valuations.

3. Insurance Underwriting Volatility & PacifiCorp Wildfire Liability

Medium Severity

FY2025 insurance underwriting profit fell roughly 20% to $7.26B, and Geico's Q1 2026 earnings declined 34% YoY even as overall underwriting improved on lower catastrophe activity. Separately, Berkshire Hathaway Energy's PacifiCorp subsidiary carries cumulative estimated probable wildfire losses of approximately $2.9B through March 31, 2026, with roughly $577M still unpaid, and an Oregon Court of Appeals decision reversing and remanding an earlier class-action verdict has introduced renewed legal uncertainty. Either a deterioration in Geico's competitive position in auto insurance or an adverse outcome in the PacifiCorp litigation could pressure segment earnings beyond what is currently reserved.

Timeline: Near-to-medium term — Geico trends will be visible in the Q2 2026 report (Aug 3, 2026); PacifiCorp litigation timeline is less predictable and could extend over multiple years.

2.5 Bull Case vs. Bear Case

Bull Case

$570 (18-24mo)

Greg Abel demonstrates that the "Berkshire system" — decentralized operations plus disciplined, opportunistic capital allocation — survives the leadership transition intact, and the market gradually restores a meaningful portion of the valuation premium lost since mid-2025.

Key assumptions
  • A market pullback (Buffett Indicator normalization from 226.8%) allows Abel to deploy a substantial portion of the $397B cash hoard into either a large acquisition or expanded equity stakes
  • Geico underwriting stabilizes and insurance underwriting profit recovers toward FY2024 levels (~$9B annualized)
  • Buyback program scales meaningfully beyond the initial $234M as management signals shares trade below intrinsic value
  • PacifiCorp wildfire litigation resolves without material incremental reserve charges
Implied path

Operating earnings recover toward $55-60B annualized run-rate (vs. $44.5B in FY2025), supporting a re-rating of P/B toward 1.55-1.65x on a larger book value base.

Requirements to play out

Visible, sizeable capital deployment within 12 months; at least two consecutive quarters of operating-earnings beats; stabilization (not just one-quarter improvement) in Geico underwriting.

Bear Case

$440 (18-24mo)

The "Oracle premium" continues to leak out as the market concludes that a $1+ trillion conglomerate without Buffett's capital-allocation halo is structurally worth a lower multiple of book value — and the cash hoard becomes a persistent drag rather than a strategic asset.

Key assumptions
  • Cash and short-term Treasuries continue climbing past $400-450B with no transformative deployment over the next 12-18 months
  • Geico's 34% earnings decline proves structural (competitive pricing pressure, market-share loss) rather than a one-quarter anomaly
  • PacifiCorp wildfire liabilities escalate beyond the ~$2.9B cumulative estimate following adverse litigation outcomes
  • Operating earnings growth remains lumpy and below the FY2024 $47.4B level on a trailing basis
Implied path

P/B compresses toward 1.2-1.3x (low end of recent historical range) on a roughly flat book value base, consistent with a conglomerate-discount re-rating absent fresh catalysts.

Requirements to play out

Continued sequential cash growth without deployment through year-end 2026; a third consecutive quarter of insurance underwriting weakness; no resumption of meaningful buybacks.

Berkshire Hathaway currently trades at $487, roughly 5.8% below its 52-week high of $516.85 and approximately 6.9% below the $520.33 consensus price target, with a P/B ratio of 1.40x sitting near the midpoint of its multi-year range. The stock's modest discount to consensus and to its own historical book-value multiple appears less a function of operating deterioration — operating earnings rose 18% YoY in the anchor quarter — and more a function of a market-wide re-rating of the "Buffett premium" following the CEO transition completed on January 1, 2026. The dislocation, where one exists, is therefore a sentiment-driven rather than fundamentals-driven gap, which historically has been a setup where Berkshire's underlying intrinsic value (book value growing at a high-single to low-double-digit rate) outpaces near-term share-price moves.

From a fundamental quality standpoint, the franchise remains intact: a $176.9B insurance float funding a diversified set of cash-generative operating businesses (insurance, rail, regulated energy, manufacturing, distribution, and retailing), a fortress balance sheet with $397.4B in cash and short-term Treasuries, and a decentralized management structure that has continued to function through the leadership change. The FY2025 operating-earnings decline of roughly 6% and the Q4 2025 underwriting weakness represent real, but largely cyclical, headwinds — insurance underwriting profitability has historically moved in multi-year cycles tied to catastrophe frequency, and the Q1 2026 partial recovery (ex-Geico) is consistent with that pattern reasserting itself.

The primary risk acknowledged in this verdict is execution risk around capital allocation under Greg Abel. Fourteen consecutive quarters of net equity selling and a record cash position that grew even after the OxyChem acquisition suggest that the most differentiated source of Berkshire's historical outperformance — opportunistic, large-scale capital deployment — has yet to be convincingly demonstrated by the new CEO. CFRA's explicit caution about Abel's lack of a public investment-management track record is a reasonable concern that the market is pricing, evidenced by the stock's underperformance relative to the S&P 500 since the May 2025 succession announcement and the unusually sharp -4.9% reaction to the Q4 2025 miss.

Near-term catalysts to monitor include the Q2 2026 earnings report (expected August 3, 2026), which will be the first full quarterly print of the Abel era without a Buffett-authored narrative cushion and will be closely watched for (i) whether Geico's underwriting trend stabilizes, (ii) whether the cash balance shows its first sequential decline as deployment accelerates, and (iii) whether the initial $234M buyback program scales meaningfully. Additional 13F disclosures will also reveal whether the Q1 2026 shift toward Alphabet and away from legacy holdings such as Bank of America continues, which would offer further evidence of an evolving — rather than simply inherited — investment philosophy under the new leadership.
